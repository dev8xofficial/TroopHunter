## 9 — The Revenue Lie: Why Your $3M Target Is Worthless Without a Profit Number

url: https://clearresults.co/playbooks/revenue-lie-profit-planning
type: article
meta: Most contractors doing $3M have no idea what they actually make. Learn how to work backward from profit and build pricing that sticks.

### Overview

Most contractors doing $3M in revenue have no idea how much money they actually make — and some are one bad month away from a cash crisis. This playbook shows how to flip from chasing revenue to engineering profit.

**Case example:** A $2.1M foundation repair company was hitting revenue targets every week with 12 crews running. Actual profit: $48K on $2.1M in revenue, after two years of 60-hour weeks.

The root cause was not a revenue problem. It was a Profit Engine problem — the same constraint found in 8 out of 10 home service businesses between $2M and $10M.

### The Constraint: You're Tracking the Wrong Number

Revenue is on the Jobber dashboard. It's what peers benchmark against. So owners optimize for revenue — taking jobs that don't fit margins, hiring crews without scoping costs, letting collection lag creep. The top line grows. The bank account doesn't.

### Five Ways This Is Bleeding You Right Now

#### 1. The Margin Misalignment
A restoration company had $120K in annual sales commissions classified as overhead instead of cost of goods. On paper, gross margin was 52%. In reality: 48%. That 3-point gap was $90K in phantom profit — undetected for two years because nobody was tracking job-level costs.

#### 2. The Collection Lag Trap
A foundation repair company booked $400K/week in revenue. Only $280K was collected — 35–40% of cash was stuck in receivables with customers paying 2–4 weeks late. One missed week from a payroll crisis despite looking successful on the books.

#### 3. The Scope Creep Bleed
An HVAC owner committed to a contractor at $1,500/month — a $18K annual bet — with no written scope defining deliverables. No scope. No accountability. No leverage.

#### 4. The Pricing Guess
Jobs are priced on feel and competitor benchmarking, with no knowledge of actual delivery cost. Half hit margin, half don't — and it's discovered weeks later, too late to fix.

#### 5. The Single-Point Collapse
A plumbing company at $2.2M had 80% of revenue dependent on one producer. If that person leaves, revenue doesn't dip — it craters.

### The Profit Flip: Five Steps

1. **Start With Your Profit Number** — Write down the actual dollar amount needed annually after taxes, debt service, and reinvestment. Not salary (that's an expense). Real profit.
2. **Calculate Your Required Gross Margin** — If fixed overhead is $800K and profit target is $400K, you need $1.2M in gross profit. At $3M revenue, that's a 40% margin. Make decisions from real numbers.
3. **Install Job-Level Cost Tracking** — Every dollar of labor, materials, and subcontractor cost gets coded to a specific job. Tools like Jobber, ServiceTitan, and JobTread already support this. The install is a discipline change, not a technology change.
4. **Build a Weekly Profit Scorecard** — Track: revenue (weekly and YTD), total COGS, gross profit dollars and margin %, collection rate, and days cash on hand. 15 minutes a week catches margin compression before it becomes a crisis.
5. **Set a Minimum Margin Rule** — "We don't bid jobs below 38% gross margin. Period." Give estimators standing authority to flag any quote below threshold. One client who installed this rule repriced 8 quotes in Q1 — and lost zero customers.

### Case Study: From $48K to $147K on the Same Revenue

**Business:** $2.1M foundation repair company. 12 crews, 2 estimators, 3 office staff.

**The problem:** Gross margin was 38%, not the 45% the books showed. Labor was misallocated. Material pricing hadn't been updated in two years. Crew efficiency was declining with no measurement in place. Owner had fired himself from payroll for three months because cash was tight — despite the income statement showing profitable.

**What was installed:**
- Reclassified $150K in costs to reveal true margin
- Built a one-page job completion form: labor, materials, subs, revenue, margin %
- Set profit target: $300K annually
- Created a pricing floor: anything under 40% gets flagged before submission
- Installed a 19-day collection target with a follow-up call at day 14

**Result (6 months, same $2.1M revenue run rate):**
- Gross margin averaged 41% (up from 38%)
- Owner profit: $147K (up from $48K)
- Collection lag: dropped from 21 days to 16, freeing $60K in cash

### Key Takeaways

- Revenue is an output, not an input. Start with the profit dollar amount you need and work backward.
- True gross margin is hidden until you track job costs. A one-page job completion form is the difference between guessing and knowing.
- Cash and profit are not the same thing. Track collection lag weekly — if it exceeds 21 days, revenue growth will make it worse.
- Pricing rules prevent margin erosion. Set a minimum gross margin and enforce it on every quote.
- Growth without a profit target just builds a bigger cost machine. Fix the profit math first, then scale.
