## 2 — 3 Things Billy Garrett Jr Does To Have A Better Lifestyle With Sickle Cell

url: https://www.sc101.org/3-things-billy-garrett-jr-does-to-have-a-better-lifestyle-with-sickle-cell/
type: article
date: 02/29/2024
author: Nnenna Kumenda
meta: Billy Garrett Jr shares three lifestyle tips for managing sickle cell disease.

### Tip 1: Diet

Improving diet is "trial and error" — figuring out which foods cause crises and which make the body feel better. Billy recommends "oxygenating" the body through food, noting that cucumbers and tomatoes are water-rich and aid hydration. His personal diet includes high vegetable intake, a gallon of water a day, lots of salad and fruit.

### Tip 2: Exercise

Keeping the body active is beneficial even in small amounts — a 10-minute walk, swimming, yoga, group hikes, tennis, or a dance class. Exercise doesn't need to be intense to be effective.

### Tip 3: Mental Health

Mental health is often overlooked in the sickle cell community. Breaking out of a "limited mindset" imposed by society can involve diet, exercise, meditation, words of affirmation, journaling, therapy, and connecting with friends and loved ones.

meta: Versetta Brown discusses family planning, pregnancy, and parenting with sickle cell disease type SS.

Versetta Brown grew up fearing pregnancy due to sickle cell (HbSS). Her partner tested negative for sickle cell disease and trait. ### What Is Sickle Cell Disease?

Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder where red blood cells transform from round and flexible to fragile, sticky, and sickle-shaped, blocking blood flow and restricting oxygen. Complications include severe pain, organ damage, and other serious conditions. Approximately 100,000 people are affected in the US; millions more worldwide, most commonly among people of African descent.

## 9 — Cass's Travel Items When Flying With Sickle Cell Disease

url: https://www.sc101.org/travel-when-flying/
type: article
date: 04/11/2023
meta: Travel guide for people living with sickle cell disease (hbSS) when flying.

Written by Cass, who lives with sickle cell disease (HbSS).

### Medical Equipment

- Request a High Altitude Simulation Test (HAST) from your doctor to assess oxygen needs at altitude.
- Portable Oxygen Concentrator (POC) — requires a prescription; may be rented or covered by insurance. Bring nasal cannula, charging cords, extra batteries, and a doctor's note.
- Power bank — to keep POC and devices charged.
- Pulse oximeter — to monitor oxygen saturation during flight.
- Wearable (e.g., Apple Watch) — for general health tracking.

### Staying Healthy

- Medications — carry in personal item, not checked baggage.
- Snacks — to take medications with food.
- N95 mask — to reduce germ exposure.
- Disinfectants.
- Water bottle — flying is dehydrating; plan for extra bathroom breaks.

### Travel Clothes (Dress In Layers)

Shirt, sweater/sweatshirt, jacket/coat, hat, scarf, compression socks (aids blood circulation), comfortable shoes.

### Staying Warm

Hot water bottle, electric hand warmers.

### Pro Tips

- Use roller luggage to conserve energy — do not carry bags.
- Travel with a disability placard if renting a car; US states and many countries honor each other's placards.

## 11 — Hydroxyurea Management In Patients With Sickle Cell Anemia

url: https://www.sc101.org/hydroxyurea-management-in-patients-with-sickle-cell-anemia/
type: article
meta: Expert overview of hydroxyurea treatment for sickle cell anemia.

Sickle cell anemia is caused by a mutation turning flexible red blood cells into rigid, banana-shaped cells that clog blood vessels, causing severe pain and organ damage. Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) protects against sickling; it decreases after birth, which is why symptoms typically appear after 6 months of age.

### How Hydroxyurea Works

Hydroxyurea increases HbF levels, decreases red blood cell stickiness, extends red blood cell lifespan, and reduces white blood cells that contribute to vessel blockage.

### Evidence and Approval

- FDA-approved for adults with sickle cell anemia in 1998 following a study showing decreased hospitalizations, reduced acute chest syndrome, and fewer transfusion requirements.
- Subsequent pediatric studies confirmed safety and similar benefits in children and infants.
- Multiple studies show hydroxyurea extends life expectancy.
- Current recommendation: offered to children as young as 9 months, including those without symptoms.

### Side Effects and Monitoring

- Decreased white blood cells and platelets — requires blood count monitoring every 2–4 weeks initially, then every 2–3 months when stable.
- Dose is started low and increased every 8 weeks to find the ideal level.
- Occasional nail darkening; rare nausea, vomiting, rash, or diarrhea.
- Appropriate contraception recommended due to theoretical fetal risk.
- No evidence of increased leukemia risk after 20+ years of use.

Anyone with HbSS or HbSb0-thalassemia should discuss hydroxyurea with their hematologist.

## 14 — Wanda Williams's Take: My Experience With Taking Hydroxyurea For Sickle Cell Disease

url: https://www.sc101.org/wandawilliams/
type: article
meta: First-person account of a 72-year-old woman's experience with hydroxyurea treatment for sickle cell disease.

Wanda Williams was not diagnosed until age 19, after the birth of her son, though she had experienced pain episodes throughout childhood managed with heat, massage, and rest. Blood transfusions were not offered until her 50s, and hydroxyurea was introduced as an alternative to a permanent line at age 55–60.

After starting hydroxyurea, she has had no further pain crises and has not been hospitalized for pain in 10–15 years. Side effects included leg and arm hair loss and nail discoloration. She monitored labs every four weeks initially, now every 2.5–3 months. She calls hydroxyurea the best thing that happened to her and advises others to try it — if it does not work, stop, but the benefits outweighed all negatives for her.

At 72, she holds a master's degree, had a 30–35 year career with a school district, has been married 34 years, and has 17 grandchildren and 3 great-grandchildren.

## 15 — Types Of Nurses That Care For Sickle Cell Patients

url: https://www.sc101.org/nursing/
type: article
author: Dr. Keith Quirolo
meta: Overview of the different nursing roles involved in caring for sickle cell patients.

Bedside nurses have the most direct patient contact, caring for 2–6 patients depending on care level. They administer medications, monitor side effects, manage blood products, support exchange transfusions, communicate between shifts, and interface with families.

### Nurse Types

- **RN (Registered Nurse):** Two general types:
  - *BSN* — 4-year degree; can specialize, supervise, and advance within hospital systems.
  - *ADN* — 2–3 year community college program; generally bedside-only without further training.
  - All RNs pass the same state licensing exam.
- **LPN/LVN** — Licensed Practical / Vocational Nurse.
- **CNA** — Certified Nursing Assistant.
- **NP (Nurse Practitioner)** — Advanced degree starting at BSN, advancing to MSN or PhD; 6+ years of college. Classified as Advanced Practice Nurse (APRN).
- **APRN** — Includes nurse anesthetists (CRNA) and Doctors of Nursing Practice (DNP).

## 16 — Fertility And Reproductive Health In Sickle Cell Disease Feat. Teonna Woolford

url: https://www.sc101.org/teonnawoolford/
type: article
date: 03/15/2022
author: Halimat Olaniyan
meta: Profile of Teonna Woolford, founder of SC RED, on reproductive health and sickle cell disease.

Teonna Woolford is Founder and CEO of SC RED (Sickle Cell Reproductive Health Education Directive). At a young age she chose bone marrow transplantation with a condition: she wanted to preserve fertility. She was told she was already infertile from the disease and its treatment. Her body rejected the transplant; she still has HbSS and is no longer fertile.

SC RED provides community, support, and educational resources for all genders and the LGBTQ+ community, covering topics including priapism, effects of treatments on fertility, and access to reproductive healthcare. Egg freezing can cost upwards of $10,000 — a significant barrier for SCD patients already managing high medical costs. SC RED works toward grants to help preserve biological parenthood options for those who desire them.

## 17 — Victor Fadhili's Take: Managing Sickle Cell Disease — A Young Adult Living In Kenya

url: https://www.sc101.org/victorfadhili/

date: 09/16/2021
meta: First-person account of managing sickle cell disease in Kenya, with a parent's perspective.

Victor Fadhili was diagnosed at 6 months after his parents traveled far for a diagnosis. In Kenya, there is no insurance support for SCD, few specialists, limited awareness, and pain medications require bargaining with doctors. At home, pain is managed with parental massage and lab monitoring.

Day-to-day management: proper diet, staying warm, avoiding exertion, and listening to his body. He experiences yellow eyes and delayed growth, and finds it difficult to disclose his condition to peers due to fear of stigma and association with witchcraft. ## 18 — Dr. Carolyn Rowley's Take: Healthy Living To Manage Sickle Cell

url: https://www.sc101.org/carolynrowley/
type: article
date: 09/24/2021
meta: Dr. Carolyn Rowley shares her lifestyle-based approach to managing sickle cell disease without pain medication.

Dr. Carolyn Rowley (HbSS, PhD, age 60) has never taken pain medication. She has been vegan since third grade and wrote a book on nutrition and sickle cell. Her lifestyle is "beyond vegan" — natural fabrics (linen and cotton), music, dancing, nature, boundaries against negativity, prayer, and stress management through breathing.

### Pain Management

During crises: heating pad, oxygen, hydration, prayer network. For sudden crises: hydration, oxygen, heat, physician contact, and if admitted, intramuscular pain medication (not IV). She stresses calm as a tool for getting through pain.

### Lifestyle Principles (Alfred Adler-based)

1. **Life task** — Engage in meaningful work or volunteering; avoid idleness.
2. **Social** — Maintain healthy, supportive relationships with clear boundaries.
3. **Love** — Healthy intimate relationships (married 24 years).
4. **Self-acceptance** — Accept the disease without blame; use it as an opportunity to educate.
5. **Spirituality** — Believe in something beyond yourself.

Additional tips: plant-based diet; eliminate caffeine, carbonated drinks, processed food, refined sugars; attend to mental health balance; find a doctor and facility that respects and trusts you.

## 20 — Sickle Cell And Pulse Oximeter Accuracy

url: https://www.sc101.org/sickle-cell-and-pulse-oximeter-accuracy/
type: article
author: Dr. Stephen Boateng
meta: Explanation of FDA warning on pulse oximeter accuracy and its implications for people with sickle cell disease.

In late February (year not specified in source), the FDA issued a safety communication warning that pulse oximeters have limitations and risk of inaccuracy under certain conditions. A December study in the *New England Journal of Medicine* found Black patients had nearly three times the frequency of undetected low oxygen levels (occult hypoxemia) compared to white patients.

### Key Recommendations

- Do not rely solely on a pulse oximeter to assess health or oxygen levels.
- Follow your healthcare provider's recommendations on frequency of checks.
- Follow manufacturer instructions for use.
- Factors affecting accuracy: poor circulation, skin pigmentation, skin thickness, skin temperature, tobacco use, nail polish.
- For best results: hand warm and relaxed, held below heart level, nail polish removed, remain still, wait for a steady reading.
- Log readings with date and time to track trends.
- Watch for signs of low oxygen: bluish coloring in face/lips/nails, shortness of breath, worsening cough, restlessness, chest pain or tightness, fast pulse.
- Report device problems via the FDA MedWatch Voluntary Reporting Form.

## 29 — The Many Faces Of Sickle Cell Pain

url: https://www.sc101.org/the-many-faces-of-sickle-cell-pain/
type: article
meta: Expert overview of sickle cell pain types: acute, chronic, and neuropathic.

Sickle cell pain syndromes involve three types — acute, chronic, and neuropathic — occurring sequentially or simultaneously with age. Acute pain begins in infancy, is unpredictable, and requires parenteral opioids in the ER or hospital. Chronic pain occurs between acute episodes and is usually managed at home with oral analgesics. Neuropathic pain can evolve from persistent chronic pain.

### Key Clinical Facts

- About 50% of acute chest syndrome cases occur within a few days of a pain crisis hospital admission.
- Sudden death can occur during or shortly after a pain crisis.
- Frequency distribution: ~30% have rare/no episodes; ~50% have occasional episodes; ~20% have weekly or monthly episodes requiring medical attention.
- Pain frequency increases in the late second decade of life and decreases after the fourth decade.
- More than 3 episodes per year is associated with reduced life expectancy.
- About 20% of patients discharged after a crisis had recurrent crises requiring treatment within one week.
- Rebound thrombocytosis and elevated fibrinogen levels after crisis resolution indicate a hypercoagulable state that may trigger recurrence.

Early, aggressive treatment to abort a crisis may prevent downstream complications.

## 30 — Transplant Versus Current Standard Of Care For Sickle Cell Disease

url: https://www.sc101.org/transplant-versus-current-standard-of-care-for-sickle-cell-disease/
type: article
meta: Expert comparison of bone marrow transplant versus medical therapy (hydroxyurea) for sickle cell disease in children.

Traditional myeloablative bone marrow transplant using a matched sibling donor has been the primary curative option for children with SCD, but carries risks including severe chronic graft-versus-host disease and sterility.

A 2015 multicenter trial (Dr. Allison A. King et al., 18 centers, 43 SCD children and 9 with thalassemia) used reduced-intensity immunoablative conditioning (alemtuzumab, fludarabine, melphalan) with matched sibling donors. Results: 93% SCD survival, 90.7% event-free survival at median follow-up of 3.42 years. No graft-versus-host disease or rejection after year one.

### Limitations Preventing It From Becoming Standard of Care

- Median follow-up of 3.4 years is insufficient to assess long-term complications such as infertility.
- No comparison group receiving current medical therapy.

### Current Medical Therapy Context

Two large pediatric studies provide comparison:
1. Prospective: 185 children on hydroxyurea (median 10.3 years) showed 15-year survival above 98%.
2. Retrospective: 1,033 children over ~6.7 years showed 5-year survival above 98%.

**Conclusion:** The King et al. trial advances the field but does not yet justify replacing current medical therapy as standard of care. A future trial needs longer follow-up, end-organ disease evaluation, fertility assessment, and a medical therapy comparison group.

## 31 — Vaso-Occlusive Pain Episodes In Sickle Cell Disease And Menstruation

url: https://www.sc101.org/vaso-occlusive-pain-episodes-in-sickle-cell-disease-and-menstruation-is-there-a-common-connection/
type: article
meta: Research overview on the connection between menstruation and vaso-occlusive pain in women with sickle cell disease.

### Background

SCD advances over the past 40 years — newborn screening, improved management of pain and acute chest syndrome, stroke detection, and vaccination — have transformed it from a "disease of childhood" into a chronic "disease of adulthood."

### Clinical Evidence

Strong evidence indicates adolescents and women with SCD experience vaso-occlusive pain (VOPAM — vaso-occlusive pain associated with menstruation) immediately before or during their periods. The largest SCD natural history study found women have more vaso-occlusive pain than men throughout their lives. Studies suggest women using contraception have lower vaso-occlusive pain rates, possibly due to menstrual cycle regulation. The biological mechanism is not yet understood.

### Hormonal Context

Estrogen is elevated in the follicular phase; progesterone in the luteal phase. When progesterone drops and the uterine lining sheds, menstruation occurs. Research is exploring how these hormonal shifts relate to vaso-occlusion.

### Preliminary Data

Pediatric sickle cell clinic data suggests up to 50% of adolescents experience VOPAM.

## 50 — Sickle Cell Trait

url: https://www.sc101.org/sicklecelltrait/

meta: Resource hub for sickle cell trait education, including fact sheets, videos, and Sicklepedia entries.

### What Is Sickle Cell Trait?

Sickle cell trait (SCT) means inheriting one sickle cell gene. SCT cannot develop into SCD. Carriers can pass the gene to their children. Most people with SCT do not experience health complications, though evolving research identifies potential complications under certain triggers. An estimated 1 in 12 African Americans have SCT; over 300 million people worldwide carry the trait.

### Resources Available

- Toolkit on important SCT topics.
- Fact sheets: "What is Sickle Cell Trait?" and "Athletics & Sickle Cell Trait."
- Video library: common questions, public figures with SCT, malaria connection, trivia (Parts 1 & 2).
