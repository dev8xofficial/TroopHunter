Post 2

One thing that often surprises people: for those living with hemolytic anemias, fatigue doesn’t improve with sleep or rest. 

It’s not just physical exhaustion, it’s also mental fatigue, emotional fatigue that shapes daily life for people with hashtag#sicklecell, hashtag#thalassemia, and hashtag#pyruvatekinasedeficiency.

That’s why it was such an honor to speak at American Society of Hematology hashtag#ASH25 this year on the importance of understanding fatigue. 

On a panel with Agios Pharmaceuticals’ Chief Medical Officer, Dr. Sarah Gheuens, I presented the interim qualitative results from the Red Cell Revolution’s Fatigue Project.

What we found underscores what the community has been saying for years: fatigue is far more complex than we often assume, and there is still significant work ahead to fully understand and meaningfully treat it. 

Thank you to the team at Agios for the opportunity to present this critical work. I’m incredibly proud of the insights this project is generating, and even more committed to advancing this conversation.

For over a decade, Ask the Experts (Live) has been one of our most impactful and empowering programs at Sickle Cell 101. We were the first to create a real-time, accessible platform where individuals impacted by sickle cell, could ask their questions and get answers directly from leading experts, no matter what part of the world they were in.

This resource has become a vital lifeline for people without access to sickle cell specialists, with participants tuning in from Africa, Europe, Asia, the Caribbean, and South America. It represents what what patient-provider engagement should look like.

I’m incredibly proud to co-host this initiative with our trusted expert and SC101 Medical Director, Dr. Keith Quirolo.

When building clinical trial protocols, many providers and researchers don’t understand the impact of bloodwork and medical testing when it comes to a patient’s decision to join a clinical trial. Getting pricked is often thought of as trivial — it’s not!

Yesterday, I had the pleasure of presenting “Factors Driving Participation in Clinical Trials Among People with Sickle Cell Disease Differ in Importance Across Geographic Regions: Findings From the Global LISTEN Survey” at the 5th Global Congress on Sickle Cell Disease in Abuja, Nigeria.

I appreciate how this poster highlights key factors that influence a patient’s willingness and ability to participate in research.

Many thanks to the Novo Nordisk team for the opportunity to share these insights as a patient advocate. We live the data and it’s powerful to see our lived experiences reflected in it to inform research and guide our own personal advocacy journies.
