## 13 — Research & Case Studies
url: https://www.wisdomcircuits.com/resources/research

### Research Approach

Wisdom Circuits works with educational institutions to conduct rigorous studies measuring the impact of the platform on learning outcomes, teacher effectiveness, and student engagement. Studies are conducted in partnership with researchers at leading universities, with strict data privacy adherence and transparent publication of both positive and challenging findings.

### Research Papers

#### Closing Achievement Gaps: Longitudinal Analysis in Diverse K-12 Settings
42 schools, 3,500 students, 3 years. Key findings: 41% narrowing of achievement gaps for ELL students; 37% improvement for lower SES students; 28% higher assignment completion for special education students.

#### Beyond Engagement: Measuring Critical Thinking Development
28 middle and high schools. Students with Wisdom Circuits access scored 27% higher on critical thinking assessments; 42% greater improvement in identifying logical fallacies; 68% of 78,000+ student-companion interactions involved higher-order thinking rather than simple fact retrieval.

#### Course-Specific Companions and Academic Performance Across STEM and Humanities
17 universities, 214 courses, 11,850 students. Key findings: 31% reduction in DFW rates; 43% improvement in passing rates for gateway calculus courses; 37% higher retention for first-generation students; significant after-hours usage between 10PM–2AM.

#### Scaling Personalized Instruction: Faculty Perspectives
176 faculty across 23 institutions. Key findings: 67% reduction in time spent answering repetitive questions; 28% increased curriculum coverage; more personalized student interactions during office hours. Three implementation models identified: Extension Mode, Reinforcement Mode, and Preparatory Mode.

#### The Silent Majority: Understanding Classroom Participation Barriers
5,000+ K-12 and higher education students surveyed. Key findings: ~65% regularly choose not to ask questions despite confusion; social anxiety and fear of peer judgment were primary reasons (72%); students from non-English-speaking backgrounds were 2.3x less likely to raise their hand; 83% said they would be more likely to ask questions in a private digital format.

#### Absence and Academic Achievement
12,000 students across 85 school districts. Key findings: Students missing 10% of school days scored 12 percentile points lower on standardized assessments; negative impact of absences was 40% greater in sequential subjects; traditional absence recovery showed only 30–45% effectiveness; Course Companion tools showed 78% effectiveness in helping students recover from absences.

#### Personalization at Scale: Impact on Student Outcomes
94 classrooms. Key findings: 23% improvement in assignment completion rates; 18-percentile-point increase in assessment scores for previously struggling students; 62% reduction in teacher time spent on repetitive questions; 37% increase in student-reported subject confidence.

#### The Relationship Between Student Participation and Student Learning
Peer-reviewed study exploring how different forms of participation impact learning outcomes and barriers to verbal engagement.

### Case Studies

#### Why Course-Specific Course Companions Are Transforming Education
Students who never raised their hands are now deeply engaging with material. Course-specific companions guide students through the learning process and reinforce critical thinking rather than shortcuts.

#### Breaking the Silence: How Course Companions Can Help Quiet Students Thrive
Up to 60% of students rarely or never volunteer to answer questions in class. Wisdom Circuits enables students to process at their own speed, reduces stigma around basic questions, and provides personalized answers tailored to specific learning styles.

#### Bridging the Absence Gap: Keeping Students on Track When They Miss Class
Students who miss just two days per month are significantly more likely to fall behind. Course Companions let students ask follow-up questions about missed material, work through examples from class, and connect missed content to prior learning.

#### Breaking Language Barriers: Multilingual Course Companions for ELL Students
Over 5 million U.S. students are English language learners (~10% of total). Circuits allow interaction in a student's native language with gradual transition to English while maintaining academic rigor.

#### From Passive to Active: Revolutionizing Homework Completion
Homework completion rates increased by 31% on average. Quality of completed assignments improved significantly. Students report reduced homework anxiety and increased confidence.

#### The Hidden Cost of Student Questions Left Unasked
24/7 access to course-specific guidance provides immediate resolution of confusion, preventing "knowledge gap cascade" and developing student persistence.

### Safety & Well-Being Policy

Wisdom Circuits is an educational tool, not a mental health or crisis intervention system.

#### Core Principles
- Circuits do not diagnose, treat, or monitor mental health or medical conditions.
- Circuits do not notify schools, parents, or third parties if concerning behavior is expressed.
- Conversations remain private within the learning environment; sensitive messages are never escalated.
- When distress or inappropriate behavior is expressed, circuits respond with empathy, encouragement to seek support from trusted adults, and referral to professional crisis resources.

#### Handling Sensitive Messages

**Off-topic questions:** Acknowledge curiosity, redirect to academic subject.

**Inappropriate language:** Remain professional, remind student to be respectful, redirect to course content.

**Mental health or threatening messages:** Acknowledge feelings with empathy, encourage immediate real-world support (trusted adult, teacher, counselor, parent), provide professional crisis resources.

Example crisis responses:
- Student: "I don't think I can do this anymore." → Circuit: empathetic response + 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline referral.
- Student: "I want to hurt myself." → Circuit: empathetic response + 988 and Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741).
- Student: "I want to hurt someone else." → Circuit: safety-first response + Crisis Text Line referral.

#### Federal Crisis Resources Referenced
- Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
- Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline: 1-800-422-4453
- National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233
- Stomp Out Bullying Helpline: 1-877-602-8559
- SAMHSA's National Helpline (substance use): 1-800-662-4357

#### Application by Account Type
- **School-provided (B2B):** Distress messages not reported to schools to avoid unmanageable legal obligations. Students always receive supportive responses and referrals.
- **Direct-to-student:** Same empathetic and resource-driven responses. No monitoring or parental notification.
- **Family accounts (parent-created):** Children's messages remain private within the platform. Parents are advised during onboarding that Wisdom Circuits is not a monitoring tool.

### AI Ethics Strategy & Cultural Framework

Wisdom Circuits implements a Four-Level Cultural Ethics Framework™ that activates distinct layers of ethical oversight depending on the highest level of the client relationship.

#### Framework Activation Logic

| Client Type | Active Levels |
|---|---|
| Ministry of Education | 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| State Department or District | 1, 3, 4 |
| Individual School or Educator | 1, 4 |

Level 1 is always active and cannot be overridden.

#### The Four Levels

**Level 1 — Platform-Level Governance (Always Active)**
Hardcoded, universal. Includes: alignment with independent AI ethics audits (e.g., ForHumanity); FERPA, COPPA, GDPR compliance; zero use of interaction data for training or commercial reuse; guardrails against cheating, plagiarism, and inappropriate content; emotional well-being checks for sensitive prompts.

**Level 2 — Ministry or State-Level Ethics**
Activated when a Ministry or State Department is the direct client. Defines national/regional boundaries for AI usage: prohibited topic categories, approved tone and language standards, alignment to national curriculum, cultural sensitivity enforcement. Higher-level restrictions cascade automatically to all subordinate institutions.

**Level 3 — District or School-Level Ethics**
Activated when a district or school is the client. Enables: creating an AI Integrity Statement; enabling/disabling tools (practice tests, essay prompts, QuickPrompts); blocking certain topics locally; reviewing flagged student activity and usage trends. District-level settings (e.g., disabling QuickPrompts during exam weeks) apply to all teachers and students in the district.

**Level 4 — Instructor-Level Settings**
Always active when an instructor is using the platform. Instructors can: define visible "Instructional Intent" for each Circuit; choose AI interaction tone (Socratic, coaching, directive, step-by-step); limit AI knowledge base to uploaded class materials; enable/disable features (flashcards, summaries, progress tracking). Cannot override Levels 1–3.

#### Hierarchy of Ethical Precedence
Level 1 > Level 2 (when Ministry is client) > Level 3 (when district/school is client) > Level 4.

#### Transparency Features
- Access to data and settings is always limited to the activated client level.
- Ethics Profile Visibility: districts and schools can display ethics configurations to families and stakeholders.
- Multilingual support enforced across all 60+ supported languages.
- Role-specific training provided for each client level.
