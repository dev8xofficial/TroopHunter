People love talking about “multiples.” “My business is worth 4× EBITDA… 6×…”

Here’s the truth:
A business is only worth its fixed assets + required working capital + whatever goodwill the returns actually justify. That’s it.


Fixed assets: a forklift is a forklift. Fair market value is obvious.
Working capital: inventory + cash you need to run the machine.
Goodwill: the premium buyers pay only when the business generates strong returns on every dollar tied up in operations.


If a company needs $1 of capital to operate and reliably returns $1.00 every year… that’s a 100% annual return. That’s a great business. Buyers will pay above book value because the cash compounding is real.

But if you’re sitting on $1M of inventory and only producing $250K of profit, that’s a 25% return. Decent? Maybe.
But does it justify paying much above the value of the inventory and assets? Not really.

It’s not rocket science.
It’s not complicated finance.
It’s simply returns on invested capital.

Talking about “multiples” without understanding fixed assets and working capital is how people fool themselves about valuation. The math never lies.
