## Lead Warming Plan — Makoto Ikegami, DSW, LCSW / Deaf Multicultural Counseling, LLC

### Analysis

#### Target CEO — Key Themes

- **"What comes after access?"** — Makoto's recurring frame is that access is the floor, not the goal; he pushes past basic accommodation toward awareness, acceptance, and alignment across different lived experiences.
- **One-size-fits-all is the enemy** — he repeatedly flags that the Deaf community is not monolithic (immigrants, DeafBlind, LGBTQ+ members, multicultural families) and that generic, off-the-shelf approaches fail the people inside the gaps.
- **Systems-level thinking over individual fixes** — language like "systemic awareness," "cross-sector collaboration," and "building strong systems and partnerships — together" shows up across both clinical and conference posts.
- **Low-ego, collective credit** — nearly every post thanks collaborators by name and frames wins as shared, not personal; he positions himself as one builder among many rather than a founder taking a victory lap.
- **Built it himself, state by state** — DMC's growth story is told as a sequence of incremental, hard-won expansions (new therapist, new state, new insurance plan), not a single big launch.
- **Identity as an outsider who still belongs** — as a Japanese-born immigrant who didn't attend a U.S. Deaf school, he frequently reflects on operating inside systems he wasn't originally built for.

#### Target CEO — Likely Pain Points

- **No ongoing technical partner** — DMC's own operations manual states the website is "built and maintained by outside freelance developers hired one project at a time, on platforms like Upwork," and names this as a gap it wants closed with "more structured, ongoing technical support" instead of one-off jobs.
- **Manual, form-based intake** — client requests, consultations, and insurance checks currently run through a basic Web3Forms request form and staff inbox rather than a connected intake or scheduling system.
- **State-by-state credentialing overhead** — DMC operates across 8 states, each with its own licensing and insurance-acceptance rules, and announces each new state individually rather than through any visible systemized rollout.
- **Founder is the de facto tech and marketing department** — there is no in-house technical or marketing team; Makoto personally drives brand, content, and contractor relationships on top of a clinical caseload, two teaching roles, and ADARA leadership.
- **Complexity inside a "specialized" niche** — DMC already serves a narrow population (Deaf-led ASL therapy), yet within that niche it must still differentiate for DeafBlind clients, immigrants, hearing family members, and multiple insurance/state combinations — exactly the kind of fragmented requirements generic software tends to ignore.

#### Dev8X — Relevant Overlap

- **Multi-tenant, white-label architecture** — Dev8X's monorepo of isolated microfrontends is a working answer to DMC's actual structural problem: one practice, eight states, eight sets of licensing and insurance rules, each needing slightly different logic.
- **Continuous engineering partner, not gig-by-gig freelancers** — Dev8X operates as an ongoing technical partner, directly addressing the exact gap DMC's own internal documentation flags about wanting steady support instead of repeated one-time Upwork hires.
- **Intake-to-delivery systems experience** — Dev8X builds dispatch, scheduling, and notification systems for service businesses; DMC's request-form → consultation → insurance-check → scheduled-session pipeline is the same shape of problem in a different industry.
- **Built for users software wasn't designed for** — Dev8X's pattern of building for non-standard, underserved markets (e.g., rural microtransit with no existing infrastructure) mirrors DMC's "no one-size-fits-all" philosophy: both have had to build the custom layer that off-the-shelf tools skip.
- **Systems-builder who also built from nothing** — Abdul's own experience growing ventures through direct relationships rather than institutional backing matches the way DMC describes its growth: organic, community-trust-driven, state by state.

#### Shared Ground

Both Makoto and Abdul are builders working at the edges of systems that weren't designed for the people they serve — Makoto removing communication and access barriers in mental healthcare for Deaf communities, Abdul removing technical and infrastructure barriers for businesses that don't fit standard software. Neither trusts a generic solution; both have built trust-based practices from very little, one relationship and one region at a time.
