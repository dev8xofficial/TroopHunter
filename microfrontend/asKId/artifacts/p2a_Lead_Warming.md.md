## Lead Warming Plan — Prof. Dr. Florian Schatz / asKId

### Analysis

#### Target CEO — Key Themes
- **AI literacy over AI dependency** — Florian repeatedly frames AI education as teaching kids to think critically about AI, not just use it.
- **"KI ohne KI" (AI without AI)** — His signature content series teaches AI concepts through offline, hands-on parent-child exercises.
- **Confidence vs. correctness** — A recurring concern that AI (and people) can sound certain while being wrong; this is pedagogical, not technical, in his framing.
- **Founder-professor duality** — He runs a full-time professorship at HAW Kiel while co-building asKId, and openly writes about bridging research and startup thinking.
- **Pride in earned credibility** — Press coverage and conference appearances are treated as validation milestones, shared with visible enthusiasm.
- **Child safety as a design premise** — DSGVO compliance and content filtering are described as foundational, not bolted-on features.

#### Target CEO — Likely Pain Points
- **Pre-traction distribution** — 24 LinkedIn followers and a two-person team mean visibility still depends entirely on founder-driven content and press hits.
- **Converting press into customers** — Page-3 newspaper coverage is a strong signal, but turning that into family sign-ups or school contracts is a separate, harder problem.
- **Bootstrap site undercutting the product** — The Upwork posting shows they know their Bootstrap website doesn't yet match the sophistication of the product.
- **Manual, case-by-case school sales** — Two of three school offers are priced "on request," meaning every school deal is a custom conversation rather than a repeatable motion.
- **Time scarcity** — Splitting time between a professorship and a startup compresses every hour available for sales, content, and product work.

#### Dev8X — Relevant Overlap
- **Edtech product experience** — Building software for education-sector buyers (schools, parents) requires credibility signals that differ from typical consumer SaaS; Abdul has navigated that gap before.
- **Design-to-conversion work** — asKId's own Upwork posting reveals a site that needs to convert better without a full rebuild — exactly the kind of scoped design problem Dev8X solves.
- **Building for a non-using buyer** — Parents and school boards are the buyers, not the end users (kids); Dev8X has worked on products where the decision-maker and the user aren't the same person.
- **Early-stage, founder-led growth** — Both companies are past having a working product and into the harder phase of building repeatable distribution.
- **Ship-fast, validate-early philosophy** — Dev8X's own build approach mirrors the lean, hypothesis-driven thinking Florian writes about applying to research teams.

#### Shared Ground
Both Florian and Abdul are technical founders who built something that works and are now wrestling with the much harder problem of getting it seen and trusted by the right buyer. That shared stage — product proven, distribution unproven — is the thread tying all three comments together.
