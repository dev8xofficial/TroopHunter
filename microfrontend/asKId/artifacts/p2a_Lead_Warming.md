## Lead Warming Plan — Prof. Dr. Florian Schatz / asKId

### Analysis

#### Target CEO — Key Themes

- **AI literacy over AI dependency** — Florian consistently argues that children need to understand _how_ AI works before using it; his posts are exercises in critical thinking, not product endorsements.
- **"KI ohne KI" (AI without AI)** — A recurring content series where he teaches AI concepts using offline, hands-on activities with children. This is his intellectual signature.
- **Epistemic humility in AI** — He writes extensively about hallucinations and the danger of AI sounding confident while being wrong — a pedagogical concern, not a technical one.
- **Founder-professor duality** — He operates simultaneously as an academic (HAW Kiel professorship) and a startup co-founder. His posts bridge research thinking and product building.
- **Lean startup mindset applied to research** — His Waterkant post shows he actively teaches scientists to think like builders: "Start building before you are ready."
- **Child safety as non-negotiable product constraint** — DSGVO compliance, content filtering, and parental controls are not features to him; they are the premise.

#### Target CEO — Likely Pain Points

- **Distribution with zero marketing budget** — With 24 followers, asKId is pre-traction. Getting the product in front of parents and schools is the existential challenge.
- **Converting academic credibility into commercial trust** — Parents and school administrators buy from brands, not professors. Florian has deep credibility in one world; translating it to the other is hard.
- **Bootstrap UI that hasn't kept pace with the product** — The Upwork job posting reveals they are actively aware that the website's design undersells the product.
- **School sales cycle complexity** — Selling to schools means navigating procurement, DSGVO sign-off, and teacher adoption simultaneously — a very long, multi-stakeholder process.
- **Time split between professorship and startup** — Running a university department and building a startup concurrently is a brutal context-switch problem that compresses every decision.

#### Dev8X — Relevant Overlap

- **Edtech product builds** — Dev8X builds software products in the education and SaaS space; Abdul has direct experience with what it takes to get a product that works technically to also feel credible to institutional buyers.
- **Design-to-conversion systems** — The job posting reveals asKId needs their Bootstrap site to convert better; Dev8X has built product-level web experiences that move visitors to action without heavy custom engineering.
- **Child-facing UX constraints** — Building for children (and their parents as the actual buyers) requires a specific design discipline — safety signals, simplicity, trust indicators — that Dev8X has navigated.
- **Pre-traction startup execution** — Both are at the stage where the product exists but distribution is the primary constraint. Abdul understands the founder pressure of this phase firsthand.
- **Lean build philosophy** — Florian's Waterkant post frames his thinking as Build-Measure-Learn. Dev8X operates the same way — ship fast, validate early, iterate with real users.

#### Shared Ground

Both Florian and Abdul are technical founders who believe that how a product is built matters as much as what it does — and both are currently in the gap between having a working product and having a market that knows it exists. The shared frustration of a credible product that hasn't yet found its distribution is the invisible thread that makes this outreach coherent.
