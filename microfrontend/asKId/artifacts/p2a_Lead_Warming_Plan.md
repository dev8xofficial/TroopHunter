## Lead Warming Plan — Prof. Dr. Florian Schatz / asKId

### Analysis

#### Target CEO — Key Themes
- **"KI-Kompetenz statt KI-Abhängigkeit"** — building real AI competence in kids, not dependency on AI.
- **"KI ohne KI"** — his recurring weekend series, teaching AI concepts (hallucinations, prompting) through offline, hands-on parent-child exercises, no screens involved.
- **Confident-but-wrong** — a running concern that AI (and people) can sound certain while being unreliable; framed pedagogically, not technically.
- **Safety and privacy as a starting principle, not an add-on** — DSGVO compliance, content filtering, and parent alerts are described as built-in from day one.
- **Founder-professor duality** — splits time between a full-time HAW Kiel professorship and asKId, and writes openly about translating research thinking into startup thinking ("Start building before you are ready").
- **Visible pride in earned credibility** — press coverage and event appearances are shared as validation, often with warm, family-inclusive framing.

#### Target CEO — Likely Pain Points
- **Pre-traction distribution** — the asKId LinkedIn page has 24 followers and two listed employees, so visibility still depends almost entirely on founder-driven posts and press hits.
- **Converting credibility into customers** — page-3 coverage across 15 regional papers is a strong signal, but turning that into family sign-ups or school contracts is a separate, harder problem.
- **No repeatable school sales motion** — two of the three school offers (teacher training, AI strategy consulting) are priced "on request," meaning every school deal is still a manual, case-by-case conversation.
- **Website undercuts the product** — their own $300 Upwork brief for a Bootstrap designer shows they already know the current site doesn't match the sophistication of the product.
- **No CRM or billing automation** — as the team grows past two people, there's no system in place to track leads, school pipelines, or family billing beyond manual handling.
- **Time scarcity** — splitting hours between a full-time professorship and a two-person startup compresses every hour available for sales, content, and product work.

#### Dev8X — Relevant Overlap
- **Scoped design-to-conversion work** — asKId has already signaled (via its own Upwork brief) that it wants a polished site without a full rebuild, exactly the kind of bounded UI/UX engagement Dev8X takes on.
- **Building a repeatable sales motion** — the two "on request" school offers are a clear opening for the kind of structured pipeline work Dev8X has built for other B2B clients.
- **Designing for a buyer who isn't the user** — parents and school boards decide, kids use the product; Dev8X has shipped for products where the decision-maker and the end user are different people.
- **Filling the missing tech layer** — no CRM or billing automation exists yet, a gap that fits Dev8X's own technical build experience as the team scales past two people.
- **Same growth stage** — both companies are past "does it work" and into the harder "how do people find and trust it" phase.

#### Shared Ground
Both Florian and Abdul are technical founders who built something that works and are now wrestling with the much harder problem of getting it seen, trusted, and bought by the right buyer. That shared stage — product proven, distribution and systems still unproven — is the thread tying all three comments together.
