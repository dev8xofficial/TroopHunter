# Code of Conduct

The Burkes Group is committed to fostering an inclusive, respectful, and collaborative environment for all contributors to our specification kit and portal. This Code of Conduct applies to all participants in our spec-kit community.

---

## Our Commitment

We are committed to providing a **welcoming and supportive environment** for people of diverse backgrounds and identities. This includes individuals of any:

- Gender identity and expression
- Sexual orientation
- Disability status
- Neurodiversity
- Physical appearance
- Body size
- Race and ethnicity
- National origin
- Age
- Religion (or lack thereof)
- Socioeconomic status
- Educational background
- Geographic location

---

## Expected Behavior

All contributors are expected to:

### 1. **Be Respectful**

- Treat all community members with dignity and respect
- Assume good intent in discussions
- Disagree thoughtfully; focus on ideas, not personalities
- Listen actively to others' perspectives

### 2. **Be Constructive**

- Provide actionable, specific feedback (not personal criticism)
- Suggest improvements alongside critiques
- Ask clarifying questions before assuming misunderstanding
- Help others succeed; mentor junior contributors

### 3. **Be Inclusive**

- Welcome newcomers and help them onboard
- Use inclusive language (avoid assumptions about gender, experience, background)
- Ensure no one feels excluded or marginalized
- Amplify voices from underrepresented groups

### 4. **Be Accountable**

- Own your mistakes and apologize sincerely
- Accept feedback gracefully
- Work to improve if called out
- Hold yourself to the same standard as others

### 5. **Respect Privacy & Confidentiality**

- Don't share others' private information without permission
- Respect confidential business information
- Use private channels for sensitive discussions

---

## Unacceptable Behavior

We do not tolerate:

- **Harassment**: Unwelcome comments, jokes, or advances of a sexual, discriminatory, or hostile nature
- **Bullying**: Persistent, deliberate attempts to humiliate, embarrass, or intimidate someone
- **Discrimination**: Exclusion or unfair treatment based on protected characteristics
- **Bad Faith Arguments**: Deliberately misinterpreting others' positions to win a debate
- **Spam or Derailment**: Off-topic posts, promotional content, or attempts to hijack discussions
- **Doxxing or Threats**: Sharing someone's private info; threats of violence or harm
- **Retaliation**: Punishing someone for reporting a violation

**Examples of unacceptable comments**:

- "People like you always X..." (generalization)
- "You're clearly not smart enough for this" (personal attack)
- "That's gay/lame/retarded" (slurs)
- "As a [identity], you should..." (stereotyping)
- "Go kill yourself" or similar threats

## Conflict Resolution

### Step 1: Direct Communication (if safe)

If someone's behavior bothers you, you may:

- Politely tell them (e.g., "That comment felt dismissive; I don't think it was intentional, but wanted to flag it")
- Give them a chance to respond
- Most conflicts resolve here

### Step 2: Report to Moderator

If direct communication fails or feels unsafe:

- Report to **Product Lead** or **Engineering Lead**
- Provide context (who, what, when, links/screenshots)
- Reporter and respondent can meet separately or together (reporter's choice)
- Moderator facilitates resolution

### Step 3: Steering Committee Review

If unresolved after Step 2:

- Case escalates to **Steering Committee** (VP Product, VP Engineering, HR if available)
- Review investigation findings
- Determine consequences (mediation, temporary ban, permanent removal)
- Communicate decision to involved parties

**Timeliness**:

- Step 1: Immediate
- Step 2: Response within 2 business days; resolution within 1 week
- Step 3: Investigation within 2 weeks; decision within 3 weeks

---

## Enforcement

Violations of this Code of Conduct may result in:

1. **Verbal Warning** — First minor violation; clear explanation of expected behavior
2. **Temporary Ban** — 30 days; violations of severity or repeat minor violations
3. **Permanent Ban** — Severe or repeated violations after warning

**All actions are proportionate to the violation and documented.**

---

## Reporting

To report a violation:

- **Email**: [contact] (confidential report to Product Lead + Engineering Lead)
- **GitHub Issue**: Open private security advisory (GitHub → Security tab → Report a vulnerability)
- **In Person**: Speak to your manager or HR representative

**What to include**:

- Who was involved?
- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Links/screenshots/context?
- Witnesses (if any)?
- What outcome do you want?

**Confidentiality**:

- Reporters are protected from retaliation
- Investigations are confidential
- Information is shared only as needed
- Complainant's identity is not disclosed in public consequences (unless they consent)

## FAQs

### Q: I made a comment that offended someone. What do I do?

**A**: If confronted:

- Listen without defensiveness
- Apologize sincerely (not conditionally: "I'm sorry you felt offended" ≠ true apology)
- Ask how to do better
- Do better

If they report it instead, follow the Conflict Resolution process.

### Q: Is this Code of Conduct only for GitHub, or also for meetings?

**A**: **Both**. It applies to all spaces where spec-kit work happens: GitHub, Slack, emails, meetings, video calls, in-person interactions.

### Q: What if I'm unsure if something is a violation?

**A**: **Report it anyway.** It's better to ask than to let potential misconduct slide. Moderators will evaluate context; not all reports result in action, but all are taken seriously.

### Q: What if the violator is a leader (PM, Architect, VP)?

**A**: **Same process.** No exceptions. If the violator is a direct manager of the reporter, escalate to their manager or HR instead of them.

### Q: Are there consequences for false reports?

**A**: **Not for good-faith reports**, even if unfounded. But deliberately false accusations are violations of this Code of Conduct and will be addressed.
