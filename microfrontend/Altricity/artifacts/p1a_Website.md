##### About

#### OUR BEGINNING

### Why Altricity Exists

I’ve been working on cars for as long as I can remember. In the 1970s, that meant helping my dad maintain our family vehicles, using his jump starter not just on our own cars, but on neighbors’ cars and even the garden tractor I used to cut lawns around the neighborhood. Dead batteries were just part of life, and jump starting was something you learned early.

For decades after that, nothing really changed. I relied on portable jump starters, jumper cables, and roadside assistance like everyone else. When a battery died, you dealt with it — usually with some inconvenience, sometimes with a long wait.

Then one day, I had a dead battery in a late-model car. The battery was weak enough that the key fob wouldn’t unlock the vehicle. Even though I had a portable jump starter inside the car, I couldn’t access it or the battery. I was locked out completely and had no choice but to call roadside assistance.

That moment was the turning point.

It wasn’t just inconvenient — it made me realize how fragile and outdated the entire jump-starting process still is. In a world of smart vehicles, mobile apps, and connected systems, we were still relying on an 80-year-old process that assumes ideal conditions, manual access, and external help at the exact moment something goes wrong.

By that point, I had spent my career building software and connected products — starting as a software engineer at Motorola and later working for more than 15 years on smart systems that combine hardware, mobile apps, vehicle telematics and cloud technologies. It was obvious to me that vehicle power recovery didn’t need another tool — it needed to be treated as a system.

I set out to design a better way, one that would stay with the vehicle, remain ready, and remove uncertainty when a battery-related failure occurs. I formed a small team, built early prototypes, and before long, Altricity was born.

What surprised us most wasn’t that the solution worked — it was how widespread the problem really is. Despite decades of technological progress in vehicles, the basic process for dealing with a dead battery hadn’t meaningfully evolved. Altricity exists to change that, by making vehicle power recovery dependable, predictable, and designed for the way modern vehicles are actually used.

Darin Minter

Founder, Altricity

##### Step 1: Mount the Altricity Jump Starter

###### Installation in the Engine Bay:

For maximum performance and reliability, the Altricity Jump Starter is designed to be installed in the vehicle's engine bay within reach of the battery using the supplied 10 AWG cables.

Choose a flat, clean surface. This could be next to the car's battery, fuse box or a suitable location in the engine bay away from direct heat and moving parts (belts, fans). Place and mount the unit at the location you have determined securely using adhesive tape, fasteners, or brackets as appropriate.

1: Altricity Jump Starter Unit installed on the side of a vehicle battery

###### Installation in other locations

If no suitable location is available in the engine bay:

- Select an alternate location agreed upon with the vehicle owner (e.g., glove box, under seats, or trunk).
- Measure the total cable length required to reach the battery.
- Upgrade the power cable to the correct gauge per the Wire Sizing Guide on page 16.
- Cut the supplied 10 AWG wire down to roughly 2 inches and solder and heat-shrink the additional length of 8 AWG wire to the Altricity Jump Starter unit.
- Route the cables securely to the battery compartment.
- Terminate with appropriate ring connectors and secure to the battery posts.

2: Example of an Altricity Jump Starter installed in on top of a fuse box.

Some models, German SUV models for example, have batteries located in alternate locations like the floorboard behind the front seats or in the interior of the rear quarter panel. These areas may allow for placement of the unit adjacent to the battery.

3: Altricity Jump Starter installed in the rear quarter panel

##### Step 2: Connect Altricity Jump Starter to the Vehicle Battery

Connect the positive lead (RED) from the Altricity Jump starter to the positive battery terminal.

Connect the negative lead (BLACK) of the Altricity Jump Starter to the negative battery terminal of the vehicle battery.

Ensure that all connections are secure.

4: Connections from Altricity Jump Starter to the vehicle battery

##### Step 3: Cable Management

Tidy up the cables using zip ties to secure them. Make sure the cables are not in contact with sharp edges, hot surfaces, or moving parts.

##### Step 4: Powering-up and Pairing

Find the power button on the Altricity Jump Starter and push it to turn the unit on.

Install the Altricity App on the customers phone from the corresponding app store (Play Store for Android and App Store for Apple). Scan the QR Code below to quickly reach download the app.

5: QR Code to download Altricity App

Once installed, open the Altricity App and sign up using your preferred method.

Fill in the details of the car such as make, model and year. Once all the details are filled in, the app will take you to the scan and connect page.

Use the Scan and Connect feature in the app to locate and pair with the Altricity Jump Starter. Follow the instructions on the screen to complete the pairing process.

Once connected and paired, verify the connection and installation by checking:

- Jump Starter Battery Level
- Vehicle Battery voltage
- Systems Information within the app.

Note: Make sure to press the PAIR Button on the Altricity Unit before attempting to pair a new mobile phone. The Altricity Jump Starter is designed to work with one phone only. Pairing a new mobile phone may prevent the unit from connecting to the previous mobile phone.

##### Step 5: Finishing Touches

Verify that the car battery voltage is displayed correctly in the app.

Additionally, we recommend checking and applying any available firmware updates to keep your Altricity Jump Starter up to date.

We recommend that you check if the clearance between the hood and unit is enough before shutting the hood hard enough to latch, which could cause damage to both the unit and the hood.
