Oil and filters.

Tire rotations.

Brakes.

Fluids.

Belts and hoses.

Scheduled inspections.

Manufacturer service intervals.

Most organizations have a plan for these because preventive maintenance works. It reduces wear, extends asset life, and helps avoid costly downtime.

But what about electrical system failures?

A weak battery.
Battery drain caused by aftermarket equipment.
A charging system issue.
An unexpected no-start condition.

These problems can develop between service intervals and often remain invisible until a vehicle is needed most.

As connected vehicles continue to evolve, there's an opportunity to look beyond maintenance schedules and ask a different question:

Is the vehicle ready to go right now?

Knowing where a vehicle is matters.

Knowing it's ready for its next assignment may matter even more.
