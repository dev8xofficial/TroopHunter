What you're building toward

Desired Outcome

Scale Integrity Dental Group so every location runs with the same consistency — replacing manual, ad-hoc management with systemized, measurable workflows. Office managers are empowered, doctors stay focused on clinical care, and you stop being the system that holds it all together.

Why Now

You've grown from one office to five-plus and adopted EOS for quarterly planning. The playbooks, scorecards, and KPIs are being written by hand right now — and you're hiring Associate Managers to scale leadership. The structure is forming this quarter, not someday.

⏱ The window is open right now. Your processes aren't hardened yet — they're being defined as you build the playbook. That makes this the rare moment when a system can shape your operations instead of disrupting them later.
Current State

The problems behind the bottleneck

#Current ChallengeBusiness ImpactPressure
1 Office managers coordinate scheduling, collections, and daily operations across 5+ locations using a manual playbook and EOS scorecards — no unified digital system shows real-time performance. Each new office adds a separate manual management stream; complexity grows with every location instead of being absorbed. Critical
2 Scheduling is "busy, not productive" — no data-driven procedure-mix balancing, provider-utilization tracking, or gap-fill optimization; schedulers rely on instinct. Provider capacity is suboptimally used, and the same gap repeats at every location without a systemized approach. High
3 Collections accountability is manual — daily targets, aging reports, and insurance follow-up are tracked without automation or standardized workflows. Collections leakage compounds across offices; financial health depends on each manager's individual diligence. High
4 Cross-location KPI visibility is fragmented — leadership can't see fill rates, production vs. collections, or team performance across all offices in one view. Strategic decisions rely on delayed, manually compiled data; an off-track office is noticed late, not early. High
5 Morning huddles and daily coordination lack automated preparation — managers manually assemble schedules, tasks, and priorities each day. Some mornings are well-prepared, others reactive; inconsistency cascades into team misalignment and patient experience. Medium
6 Onboarding for new doctors and managers is ad-hoc — no structured digital curriculum; the curriculum is being built from scratch by crowdsourcing on LinkedIn. Time-to-productivity for new hires stays long, which constrains the capacity needed to keep scaling. Medium

Looking Ahead

The gap between your goal and the bottleneck

You've said it plainly: build "the best dang dental group on the planet" — a place where every office delivers the same care, and where the organization performs even when you're not carrying it. These are the three things that quietly stand between that goal and where operations sit today.

12–24 months out
Management burden scales linearly with each new office
Without a force multiplier, opening office #6, #7, #8 means proportionally more of your time to train, monitor, and support each new manager. CEO bandwidth becomes the ceiling.
→ Stands directly between you and an organization that runs without you carrying it; at scale this gap grows, it does not shrink.
Any departure
A manager's exit walks institutional knowledge out the door
When playbooks are personal rather than digital and transferable, one key departure forces a reactive scramble — schedules slip, collections drift, and recovery takes months, not weeks.
→ Stands directly between you and consistency you can trust; the more you grow, the more single points of failure you create, not fewer.
As you scale
Patient experience drifts apart across locations
Without systemized standards, each office develops its own shortcuts and its own definition of "good enough." Reviews and word-of-mouth — your strongest growth engine — become inconsistent.
→ Stands directly between you and the reputation built over 21 years; one off-experience office can dilute it across all of them as you grow.
