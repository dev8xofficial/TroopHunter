Section 03 — What's Blocking the Primary Goal

Current Operating Challenges

Each problem below was identified directly from Dr. Smith's own public statements and operational signals. These are not generic dental practice challenges — they are the specific friction points IDG is experiencing right now.

# Current Challenge Business Impact Severity
1 No unified operations platform. Office managers coordinate scheduling, collections, and daily operations using a manual playbook and EOS scorecards — performance data is siloed in each location. Every new location adds a separate management stream. Complexity grows linearly instead of being absorbed by a central system — the primary barrier to scalable growth. Primary Block
2 Scheduling is busy, not productive. Chairs fill without data on procedure mix, provider utilization, or gap patterns — schedulers optimize on instinct across five locations. Provider time is suboptimally allocated at every location. The same inefficiency replicates across all offices because there's no data-driven baseline to improve from. Revenue Risk
3 Collections accountability is manual. Daily targets, aging reports, and insurance follow-up are tracked location-by-location without automated prioritization or escalation. Collections discipline at each office depends entirely on individual manager effort. Leakage is invisible until month-end — and compounds across five locations simultaneously. Cash Flow Risk
4 Leadership visibility is fragmented. Dr. Smith and regional leadership have no unified, real-time view of KPIs, scorecards, or performance data across all offices. Strategic decisions — where to invest, which location needs support, where to expand — rely on delayed, manually compiled data gathered office by office. Growth Risk
5 Morning huddle prep is manual and inconsistent. Office managers assemble agendas from scratch each morning — some days are well-prepared, others are reactive. Inconsistent huddle quality cascades into daily misalignment. The tone of the office day is set by how much time the manager had to prepare rather than by a reliable system. Daily Friction
6 Onboarding for new doctors and managers is ad-hoc. Dr. Smith is building the onboarding curriculum from scratch — no structured digital path exists for new hires to follow independently. New Associate Managers and early-career doctors depend on in-person shadowing and personal guidance. Time-to-productivity is long, and the knowledge lives with individuals, not the organization. Capacity Risk

Section 04 — What Breaks in 12–24 Months Without a Change

Three Risks That Grow With Every New Location

Management burden scales with you — not for you
Opening location #6, #7, or #8 without a centralized system means each new office requires proportionally more of Dr. Smith's personal time to train, monitor, and support. Growth stalls at the threshold of her personal bandwidth.
How the platform prevents this One system trains every new manager on the same framework. Dr. Smith's attention expands the group — it doesn't manage each office.
Key manager departure = operational setback
When playbooks are personal rather than digital, a single manager leaving takes the knowledge with them. Recovery after a key departure takes months — and patients notice before leadership does.
How the platform prevents this When the playbook lives in the system, not in a person's head, any departure is a staffing event — not an operational crisis.
Inconsistent experience erodes 21 years of brand equity
As IDG adds locations, each develops its own shortcuts and its own definition of "good enough." Google reviews and word-of-mouth — the group's strongest growth engine — become inconsistent across offices.
How the platform prevents this Standardized daily workflows, huddle prep, and KPI thresholds mean every patient at every location experiences the same standard of care — not the one they happen to get.
