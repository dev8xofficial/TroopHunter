## FRAMEWORK OVERRIDE (applies to entire prompt)

This prompt operates under the Decision-Led Proof Framework v2.

1. **Narrative Formula (truncated on purpose):** The video follows only the FIRST
   THREE beats of the formula — Future Outcome → Current Bottleneck → Proof of
   Understanding. It STOPS before Safe Change and Strategic Unlock. Those beats
   are the consultation's payload and must not appear in the video.
2. **Decision Emotion:** Trigger Recognition in the first 20 seconds and Trust by
   the halfway mark. Do NOT chase Relief — Relief comes from the solution, and the
   solution is withheld. Emotion labels are internal only; never printed.
3. **ROI Integrity:** Any number spoken aloud must be L1 or L2. Exactly ONE
   directional stakes number is allowed (see Hold-Back Rules). No ROI tables, no
   per-solution metrics.
4. **Confidence Signal:** Read it from the Decision Card. It controls how firmly
   the stakes number and the reply ask are phrased (see each section).
5. **Primary Source:** The Decision Card and Problem Register are PRIMARY sources.

### THE HOLD-BACK RULES (the core of the paid model — enforce strictly)

The video PROVES understanding of the problem and STOPS. It must:

**SHOW / SAY (allowed):**
- The single primary bottleneck, named in the prospect's own language.
- 3–5 current problems drawn verbatim from the Problem Register, in his words.
- The Research Transparency beat: what you can see from outside vs. what you can't
  see yet. (This beat does double duty — it builds Trust AND becomes the reason
  the consultation exists.)
- ONE directional stakes number (see below).
- ONE single-sentence proof of competence (see below).
- One gentle Fear-of-Inaction line about how the problem compounds as they grow.

**NEVER SHOW / SAY (withheld — these are the consultation's payload):**
- Any solution, feature, or "what we'd build."
- Any ROI table, operational-impact dashboard, hours-saved math, or per-problem
  metrics.
- Any build timeline, team, phases, or cost.
- Any price for the consultation, or the consultation itself. (Messaging —
  including the paid offer — happens entirely in `p3c_Messaging_Playbook.md`.)

If the source HTML brief (p3a) contains solution cards, ROI sections, or a build
timeline, the script must SKIP those screens during the walkthrough. Narrate only
the problem, research-transparency, and future-risk surfaces.

### THE ONE STAKES NUMBER

Pick the single strongest L1/L2 figure that quantifies the COST of the bottleneck
(not the value of a solution). Frame it as a rough, directional estimate — the
point is to make the eventual consultation fee look trivial by comparison, while
proving there is real money on the table.

- High Confidence: state it plainly ("you're losing roughly X").
- Medium Confidence: hedge it ("my rough estimate is X — one of the first things
  we'd confirm together").
- Low Confidence: make it a range and frame as hypothesis ("it could be anywhere
  from X to Y — that gap is exactly what the session pins down").

**Decoupling rule (critical — fixes the Beat 3 → Beat 4 seam):** the stakes number
must NOT be built from the exact unknowns you confess in Beat 3. If Beat 3 says "I
can't see your conversion rate," do not then quote a figure that secretly requires
it — a sharp CEO will catch "you said you can't see it, then priced my loss on it."
Build the number from what IS observable, frame it as "even on conservative
assumptions," and close with "the exact figure is precisely what we'd pin down
together." That turns Beat 3's honesty into the bridge instead of contradicting it.

If no defensible L1/L2 figure exists, use a directional volume statement instead
("a meaningful share of [outcome] is slipping through this gap") — never fabricate.

**This exact figure — the exact wording, not just the number — is what
`p3c_Messaging_Playbook.md` will quote back in Message 2's stakes tie-back.**
Keep it clean and quotable.

### THE ONE PROOF OF COMPETENCE

Include exactly one sentence, somewhere after the Trust beat, that shows Dev8X can
DELIVER, not just diagnose. Pull from real Dev8X context if attached (a prior
result, a comparable build) — a specific, verifiable one.

**If no real proof point is available, OMIT the line entirely.** A generic capability
statement ("this is the kind of operational gap we build systems to close") carries
no information, occupies words in an already-tight script, and reads as the one
salesman sentence in an otherwise diagnostic piece — it costs more credibility than
it earns. At this gate you are not selling build capability; you are selling
diagnostic capability, and the preceding two minutes are the proof. Never invent a
fake client or result, and never pad with a capability claim in place of one.

## THE PSYCHOLOGICAL SPINE (what actually produces a reply)

The beats below are the skeleton. These twelve rules are the nervous system. A
script can hit every beat and still land dead if it breaks these. Each is a named,
enforceable rule — check the finished script against all twelve before output.

### 1. The Open Loop Rule (the strongest opening available)

People cannot leave their own unfinished questions alone. A founder who has
publicly wondered about something is carrying an open loop, and a stranger who
closes it is impossible to ignore.

**Before writing Beat 1, search the research for the prospect's own unresolved
public question** — a post that ends in a question, an admission of something they
have just started noticing, a problem they said they were "about to go look at," a
crowdsourcing ask. That is the opening. You are not introducing a topic; you are
answering a question they already asked out loud.

> Washington Smiles has a near-perfect example. Post 16: *"once you see them, you
> can't unsee them… Anyway, off to question every process I've ever touched."* She
> announced an intention to audit her own operation. The cold open should hand her
> the audit. Post 24 is a second open loop — she asked her network to help build an
> onboarding curriculum.

If no open loop exists, fall back to naming the bottleneck directly (the current
Beat 1). But look hard first. An open loop beats a cold assertion every time.

### 2. The Assembly Rule (never tell them something, join two things they said)

A cold stranger has no standing to tell a CEO a new fact about their own business.
They have complete standing to point out that **two things the CEO already said,
months apart, are the same thing.** The prospect supplies every fact; you supply
only the join. The conclusion is then *theirs*, and people do not argue with
conclusions they reached themselves.

This is the difference between the video proving you **read** and proving you
**understood.** Reciting three of their posts back to them earns "yes, I wrote
those." Showing that post 7 and post 16 and post 9 describe one bottleneck earns
the reply.

**Enforcement:** Beat 2 must contain at least one sentence of the shape *"You've
described these as separate things. I don't think they are."* If Beat 2 is a list,
it has failed.

### 3. Mirror the Emotional Vocabulary, Not Just the Operational One

Every prompt in this system says "in the prospect's own language" and every model
reads that as *operational* language — the nouns, the process words. That produces
recognition of the problem. It does not produce the feeling of being **known**.

Founders describe their operations in nouns and their experience in adjectives. The
adjectives are what nobody else has ever said back to them. Extract both.

> Dr. Smith's operational vocabulary: playbook, scorecards, scheduling, collections,
> bottleneck. Her *emotional* vocabulary, from the same posts: it started to feel
> **"heavy."** Scaling **"exposes your blind spots."** The **"quiet cost"** of
> carrying what no one sees. Using "heavy" and "blind spot" in the script — her
> words, her register — does more work than any operational noun.

Use at most two. More than two reads as mimicry.

### 4. Index the Stakes to a Scale Event They Have Already Announced

Fear of inaction is inert when the future is abstract ("as you grow…"). It becomes
real when it is attached to a **specific commitment the prospect has already made
in public** — a hire announced, a location signed, a program launched. The threat is
then not your prediction; it is their own calendar.

> Post 21: she is hiring **Associate Manager positions across the organization**, a
> pipeline to make more office managers. Every one of those hires inherits a
> document, not a system. The stakes line writes itself, and she cannot argue with
> the date, because she set it.

**Rule:** the Beat 4 compounding line must name a scale event sourced from the
research, not a hypothetical one.

### 5. Autonomy Preservation ("But You Are Free")

Explicitly affirming a person's freedom to refuse is one of the most reliably
replicated findings in compliance research — it substantially raises compliance
rather than lowering it, because it removes the reactance that a request creates.
This is not a trick; it is what makes the ask honest.

Beat 5's reply ask must contain an explicit, sincere permission to dismiss:
*"If I've misread this, say so and I'll leave it there."* A CEO who feels free to
say no is far more likely to say something. **Never** follow the permission with a
softening clause that takes it back ("…but I really think you should look at this").

### 6. Designate the Peak Line

The peak-end effect means one sentence will carry the entire memory of the video —
and if you do not choose it, the script chooses badly by default. Every script must
nominate exactly **one Peak Line**: the sentence you would want quoted back to you
in the reply, or screenshotted to a spouse.

It is almost always the Assembly Rule sentence (#2) or the compounding line (#4).
It must be short enough to remember, contain no jargon, and survive being repeated
with no context. Print it, labelled, in the internal notes. Deliver it slower than
everything around it, with a beat of silence after.

> Washington Smiles peak candidate: *"You're building the system that's supposed to
> make the group run without you — by hand, yourself, on your own time."*

### 7. Reactance Guard: never diagnose their competence

The Decision Card's emotional-risk field is not advisory. For a founder proud of
what they built, one condescending adverb costs you the client in the first ten
seconds.

**Banned as modifiers on anything the prospect built:** *still, just, only, merely,
simply, basic, manual* (as a pejorative), *outdated, behind, actually.*
**Banned framings:** any comparison to a better-run peer or competitor; any implied
"you should have done this already"; any use of *"you're losing"* aimed at a
decision they made rather than at a gap in the world.

The problem is never *them*. The problem is that **the thing they built is a
document and the job needs a system.** Their work is the hero. Its format is the
bottleneck. That framing lets a proud CEO agree with you without conceding
anything, which is the only way a proud CEO ever agrees with a stranger.

### 8. The Identity Transition Rule (speak to who they are becoming)

Founders do not experience a bottleneck as an operational fact. They experience it
as evidence about **who they still are.** Most have publicly named the identity
they are trying to grow into, and the bottleneck is almost always the last surviving
artifact of the identity they are trying to leave.

Find that stated transition in the research and aim the diagnosis at it. Not *"your
process is manual"* — that's an insult to a competent person. Rather: *"this is the
last thing still running through the operator, in a group you've said you want to
lead."* Same fact, and now it is on their side.

> Post 9, her own words: *"the biggest shift wasn't external. It was internal —
> from operator to leader. From control to trust. From 'my practice' to 'our
> mission.'"* The manual playbook is the operator's last possession. Say that, and
> she has to reply.

### 9. The Regulatory Fit Rule (match gain/loss framing to their orientation)

The framework mandates Fear of Inaction in Beat 4 universally. That is a mistake for
roughly half of founders. Persuasion strengthens when the *frame* matches the
prospect's motivational orientation, and weakens when it fights it.

- **Promotion-focused** prospects (language of ambition, growth, building,
  possibility, "more") are moved by what the bottleneck **prevents them from
  becoming.** Frame Beat 4 as a *ceiling*, not a leak. Loss language slides off them.
- **Prevention-focused** prospects (language of duty, protection, risk, standards,
  "what we can't afford to lose") are moved by what the bottleneck **threatens.**
  Frame Beat 4 as erosion of something they built.

Read the orientation from the Decision Emotion Map (§10) and the prospect's own
verbs. Do not mix frames — a prospect gets one.

> Dr. Smith is strongly promotion-focused: *"something inside me kept pulling for
> more,"* *"the best dang dental group on the planet."* Beat 4 must therefore be a
> **capping** frame — this gap is the ceiling on the group she says she is building
> — not a "you are bleeding money" frame, which would land flat and slightly
> insult her.

### 10. The Reply Friction Rule (make the ask answerable in ten words)

The reply ask fails not because the prospect is unwilling but because the reply is
*work*. "Tell me which of these is the real problem and which I overweighted" asks a
CEO to sit down and analyse. The reply that never gets written is the reply that
required a laptop.

**The ask must be answerable from a phone, one-handed, in under ten words, without
re-watching.** Prefer a forced choice over an open question — choosing is cheap,
composing is expensive:

> "Which one did I get wrong — the scheduling read, or the collections read?"

Any reply opens the thread, and `p3c` takes it from there. You are not trying to
extract insight in Beat 5. You are trying to make the first keystroke free.

### 11. Never Bill Them For Your Effort (the indebtedness trap)

Unsolicited effort creates obligation, and obligation creates avoidance. A CEO who
feels they now *owe* a stranger a considered reply will often resolve the discomfort
by never opening the message again. Stating your investment — *"I spent an hour on
this,"* *"I built this specially for you"* — converts a gift into an invoice.

Let the artifact carry the effort. A personalised brief with their five office names
on it is self-evidently expensive; saying so out loud makes it cheap. Effort may be
stated as a plain fact about your own week, never as a claim on their attention, and
never with a number attached to it. **Delete every sentence whose function is to
make them notice how much work you did.**

### 12. Concreteness (abstractions do not survive a phone speaker)

Abstract nouns are how a script sounds professional and communicates nothing. They
are also where invented certainty hides.

**Banned in `🎙 SPEAK` blocks:** *visibility, systemization, operational efficiency,
optimization, alignment, leverage, streamline, synergy, holistic, robust,
scalability,* and any phrase of the shape *"a meaningful share of X."* If a sentence
would survive being pasted into a competitor's deck about a different company, it is
abstract — cut it.

Replace each with a thing a person does, at a time, in a place: not *"fragmented
cross-location visibility"* but *"you find out on Thursday that Tuesday went wrong."*

**Read every `🎙 SPEAK` block aloud before output.** Statements that are easy to
process are judged more true; a sentence that stumbles the tongue reads as false to
the ear even when the listener cannot say why. Also: **use the prospect's name at
most once in the entire video.** Repetition is the tell of a mail merge, and it is
the single most common way a personalised video announces that it is not.

## WHAT WE DELIBERATELY DO NOT DO (and why)

These are standard persuasion tools. They are barred from this video, and the ban is
strategic, not squeamish. The entire paid model rests on the prospect concluding
that Abdul does not behave like a salesperson. One of these undoes that in a second,
and the loss is invisible — the prospect simply never replies, and no one learns why.

- **Manufactured scarcity or deadlines** ("I only take three clients this quarter").
  A cold stranger has no standing to impose urgency, and a CEO has seen it a
  thousand times. Real urgency is *theirs* — the hire they already announced (Rule 4).
- **Unverifiable social proof** ("we've helped dozens of practices"). Dozens of
  which? A claim that cannot be checked is a claim that will be discounted, and it
  spends the credibility Beat 3 just bought. If a proof point isn't specific and
  real, omit it (see THE ONE PROOF OF COMPETENCE).
- **Reciprocity guilt.** See Rule 11. Never invoice them for the brief.
- **Fabricated precision.** "You're losing $47,300 a year" outperforms a range —
  right up until the CEO asks how you computed it. The ROI Integrity Ladder exists
  because that question ends the relationship.
- **Flattery as an opener.** It is the universal signature of the pitch that follows.
- **Any claim about their competence, their team, or their character**, positive or
  negative. Praise from a stranger who wants something is heard as leverage.

## SCRIPT FORMAT RULES

Use exactly this annotation system throughout the video script:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `🖥 SCREEN` | What is visible on screen at this moment |
| `🎙 SPEAK` | Words to read aloud verbatim (wrap spoken text in quotation marks) |
| `👆 ACTION` | Specific scroll or hover to perform |
| `📌 NOTE` | Internal note for the recorder — do not read aloud |
| `⏱ TIMING` | Approximate elapsed time marker |

**Tone rules for all 🎙 SPEAK blocks:**
- Open every screen with the PROBLEM, never a feature. (Learned from the demo
  pitch: "Right now you do X by hand" — not "here is the dashboard.")
- First person singular: "I", "I'll", "I've" — never "Abdul" or third person.
- Plain English. One idea per sentence. Peer-to-peer, CEO-to-CEO.
- No greetings, no "Hi, I'm Abdul." Start inside his problem.
- No filler: no "So basically", "As you can see", "I'd love to".
- Calm, unhurried, diagnostic — a doctor reading an X-ray, not a salesman.

**Total spoken words target:** ~280–360 words = 2–3 minutes at a calm ~120 wpm
pace. The mandated unhurried delivery means fewer words than a normal read — if the
beats run long, TRIM. Calm delivery that lands beats beats the cramming of more
words.

## PRE-FLIGHT QA GATE (mandatory — clear this before recording)

A cold CEO who hears a stranger confidently describe a WRONG thing about their
business disengages faster than from no outreach at all — and CEOs talk. So this
gate **overrides** the framework's "commercially practical / 80% is enough"
instinct for the cold first touch.

**Minimum-Specificity Threshold — do NOT produce a recordable script unless ALL hold:**
- At least **3 problems** are specific, verifiable, and traceable to a NAMED public
  source (their site, a real review, a real post) — not inferred or generic.
- The primary bottleneck is supported by real evidence, not a single weak signal.
- The stakes number has a defensible L1/L2 basis (see decoupling rule above).

**Quote Provenance Check (mandatory — every line spoken on camera):**
The Decision Card and Problem Register are PRIMARY for *judgement*, never for
*quotation*. Any sentence attributed to the prospect in a `🎙 SPEAK` block must be
re-verified against the raw `p1*` research file it cites, in this run, before the
script is written. Do not trust a quote because a prior artifact contains it.

For each spoken quote, print a provenance line in the QA gate:

```
QUOTE: "productive scheduling, not just busy scheduling"
SOURCE: p1c_Linkedin_Owner.md · Post 7 · line 228 · verified this run ✅
```

If a quote cannot be located in the raw file, do NOT silently drop it and do NOT
paraphrase around it. STOP and report the mismatch — a Decision Card whose quotes
no longer resolve is a Decision Card built on a superseded research capture.

**Research Freshness Pin.** Research files get re-scraped, and a re-scrape can
widen the net while dropping the exact recent posts the diagnosis rests on. Before
writing, confirm the p1 files still contain the posts p0a/p0b cite, by content —
not by post number. **Post numbers are not stable identifiers across scrapes.**
Cite by distinctive phrase. If the diagnosis and its source have drifted apart,
the fix is to re-run p0a/p0b against current research, never to record anyway.

If the threshold is not met, STOP and output only: "Research too thin for a
personalised first video — gather more, or use a generic warm touch." A human
reviews and approves the diagnosis before any video is recorded. Confident
wrongness is the most expensive failure in this system.

**Version guard (the script's integrity depends on this):** confirm the attached
p3a brief is the TRIMMED (hold-back) version — NO solution cards, NO ROI dashboard,
NO build timeline, and a reply-ask CTA (not a "Watch the Demo" button). If the
brief still has those sections, it was generated with an outdated p3a — STOP and
flag it. Do not rely on "skip those screens" narration: a scroll-recording will
leak them on screen even if you never speak to them.

## PART 0 — THE DM WRAPPER & DELIVERY (this is what earns the click)

The best 3-minute script is worthless if no one presses play, or plays it muted.
Produce all four of these alongside the script.

### 0.1 — The wrapper message (DM text sent with the video)
The line beside the video decides the watch rate. It must:
- **The first ~7 words are the whole game — draft them first, as a standalone
  hook.** LinkedIn's inbox row and push notification show roughly 7 words
  before the client has to open the message to see more. Whatever sits in
  those 7 words IS the pitch. Confirmed against a real client's skip
  behavior: an opener like "You've probably seen me in your comments" reads
  as filler in that window and gets skipped without opening — no hook, no
  reason to open, feels like wasted time. The 7-word opener must be specific
  to their business and curiosity-inducing — never a pleasantry, never
  relationship context, never a greeting.
- **Acknowledge the light prior contact — never claim total coldness — but
  only AFTER the hook, inside the body of the message.** By the time this
  sends, he has already seen Abdul's name across several comments on his own
  posts (Stage 0 warming) and accepted the connection request. Do NOT write
  "we've never spoken" or "this is out of the blue" — it's not true, and a CEO
  who has already seen your name will register the mismatch. This
  acknowledgment is only read once the message is opened — it must never
  occupy the 7-word preview window.
- Tease the bottleneck without giving it away — earn curiosity, don't summarise.
- Carry a light legitimacy signal (Dev8X is a real studio) so paying later isn't a
  leap from nowhere.
- **Do not price your own effort (Rule 11).** "I spent an hour looking at how you
  take on new patients" states a fact about your week and is acceptable. "I built
  this specially for you," "I've spent days on this," or any hours figure attached
  to *them* converts a gift into an invoice, and a prospect who feels indebted
  avoids the message rather than repaying it. Let the personalised brief prove the
  effort; never narrate it.
- 2–3 sentences, peer-to-peer, zero hype, no link-spam phrasing.
- Example shape: "Found something in your new patient intake. You've probably
  seen me in your comments a few times — I run a small dev studio (Dev8X) and
  spent an hour looking at how [Company] takes on new patients. Recorded a
  3-minute read of where I think it's leaking. No pitch — tell me if I'm
  wrong." — note the hook ("Found something in your new patient intake") now
  leads; the comment acknowledgment moved to the second sentence, safely past
  the preview window.
- **The 7-word-hook-first rule applies to every cold or dormant-thread DM in
  this system** — the second wrapper below, and the silent-watcher touch
  (`p3c` Branch B). Warm, active-thread replies (Message 1, Message 3 in
  `p3c`) carry less preview risk since the client already opened the
  conversation, but leading with substance over pleasantries never hurts
  there either.
- If the video is not watched 5–7 days later, produce a second, shorter wrapper
  with a different angle. Never more than 2 wrapper attempts.

### 0.2 — Captions (mandatory)
A large share of DM video is watched on mute, and the entire persuasion track is
the spoken word. The video MUST ship with **burned-in captions** of every 🎙 SPEAK
line. Output the full caption transcript as a clean block so it can be added in
editing. A muted viewer must still receive every beat.

### 0.3 — Recording for mobile (legibility)
Most prospects watch on a phone; the p3a brief is a dense, scan-first dashboard
that is unreadable as a full-page scroll on mobile video. So:
- Do NOT film a continuous full-page scroll. **Zoom into each narrated element**
  (hero stat, one problem row, the transparency table) so text is legible at phone
  size — one element on screen at a time, matched to the beat being spoken.
- Record at 1280×720 or higher; verify the smallest on-screen text is phone-readable.

### 0.4 — The LinkedIn Connection Request (before the video)
Sent after a warming comment has received engagement (a like, reply, or profile
view). No pitch — zero. It's a handshake, not a sales letter. Wait 24–48 hours
after acceptance before sending the video.

⏱ TIMING: 0:00
🖥 SCREEN: The hero of the brief — the bottleneck headline.
🎙 SPEAK: **Open on their open loop if one exists (Psychological Spine, Rule 1)** —
quote the unresolved question or intention back to them, then answer it. Otherwise:
ONE sentence naming the exact operational bottleneck in their own language, plus ONE
sentence that you noticed it from how they actually operate. No intro.
Length: 2 sentences, ~40 words.
📌 NOTE: The disarming "who I am / why this is cold" context lives in the wrapper
message (Part 0.1), NOT here — so the open stays a pure problem-first hook.
📌 NOTE: Reactance Guard (Rule 7) applies hardest here. Check the first twelve words
for any banned modifier. "The playbook you're *still* writing by hand" fails; "You
built the playbook. It's good. It's also a document" passes.

### BEAT 2 — The Homework: his real problems, in his words (Recognition deepens)
**Purpose label:** Prove this is not a template
⏱ TIMING: ~0:20
👆 ACTION: Scroll slowly down the Current Problem Register table.
🖥 SCREEN: The problem table.
🎙 SPEAK: Walk 2–3 of their real problems — **not four, and not as a list.** State
where each came from (their site, public reviews, posts). Then **execute the
Assembly Rule (Rule 2):** close the beat by joining them into one thing the prospect
has not said themselves. Frame it as honest homework on what's PUBLIC — "anyone who
looked closely would see this" — never as deep surveillance of their internal
operations, which reads as creepy. Emphasise none of it is a guess. Length: 4–6
sentences, ~90 words.
📌 NOTE: Surveillance calibration — cite public sources by name; avoid implying you
studied private systems. Specificity must feel like diligence, not intrusion.
📌 NOTE: **Recital test.** If every sentence in this beat could be answered by the
prospect with "yes, I wrote that," the beat has failed and must be rewritten. Two
quoted problems plus one earned connection beats four quoted problems and no
connection. Trim the fourth problem to buy the words for the join — the connection
is what the whole video is for.
📌 NOTE: Mirror one emotional adjective from their own writing here (Rule 3), not
only their operational nouns.
📌 NOTE: The Assembly sentence should aim at the identity transition they have named
publicly (Rule 8), not at their process. Never "your process is manual."

### BEAT 3 — The Honest Limit: the Trust beat AND the bridge to paid
**Purpose label:** The most important beat — TRUST + the reason the session exists
⏱ TIMING: ~1:00
🖥 SCREEN: The Research Transparency table (can-see vs. must-confirm).
🎙 SPEAK: Be straight about the boundary of outside research. State plainly what you
CAN see, then name the 2–3 things you CANNOT see yet (the internal numbers). Say
those unknowns are exactly where the real money is, and the first thing you'd map
together. This is the bridge — do not name a price or a session yet.
Length: 4–5 sentences, ~90 words.
📌 NOTE: **Aim the honesty outward, not inward.** A confession framed as *"my
research has gaps"* sounds like an apology and makes the prospect the judge of your
work. The same three unknowns framed as *"these are three numbers that probably
don't exist in one place anywhere in your group today"* make the gap **theirs** — and
a gap in their own instrumentation is precisely what a paid session exists to close.
Say the unknowns are unknown to you; **imply, without accusing, that they are
unknown to everyone.** Never assert that the prospect doesn't know their own numbers
— ask it as the question it is. If they DO have the numbers, they will reply to tell
you so, which is also a reply.
📌 NOTE: If the prospect has publicly used a word like "blind spot," this is the beat
to hand it back to them (Rule 3).

### BEAT 4 — The Stakes Number + the Regret Gap (Fear of Inaction)
**Purpose label:** Make the cost real, then point it at their own ambition
⏱ TIMING: ~1:40
🖥 SCREEN: One future-risk card from the brief.
🎙 SPEAK: Three moves, in this order:
1. State the single directional stakes number, phrased per the Confidence Signal.
   Apply the **Decoupling rule** (see THE ONE STAKES NUMBER): build it from what is
   observable, frame it "even on conservative assumptions," and note the exact
   figure is what you'd confirm together — so it never relies on the unknowns you
   just confessed in Beat 3.
2. **The regret gap:** name the prospect's OWN stated goal (pull the Primary Goal
   from the Decision Card §1 and the CEO's own words from the owner profile, p1c),
   then show this bottleneck is the thing standing between them and it — and that
   it compounds, it does not shrink. Example shape: "You've said you want [their
   stated goal]. Right now this one gap is the single thing capping that — and at
   [scale event] it doesn't stay the same size, it doubles."
   **The [scale event] must be one they have already announced publicly** (Rule 4) —
   a hire, a location, a launch. A generic "as you grow" is inert. A hire they posted
   about last month is a date they set themselves, and it cannot be argued with.
3. The one proof-of-competence sentence — **default it to the start of Beat 5** to
   keep Beat 4 from overloading (Beat 4 already carries stakes + regret gap; adding
   a fourth move here risks a rushed, crammed delivery).
**Tone discipline (mandatory):** Pressure their GOAL, never their character. No
guilt, no shame, no "a good leader would have…". The tension must be the gap
between their own ambition and the unaddressed bottleneck — self-generated, not
imposed. Calibrate the framing to their communication style from the Decision
Emotion Map §10 (visionary / operator / numbers-driven / mission-driven).
Length: 3–4 sentences, ~70 words.
📌 NOTE: **Apply the Regulatory Fit Rule (Rule 9) before writing a word of this
beat.** A promotion-focused founder gets a CEILING frame ("this is the one thing
capping what you said you're building"). A prevention-focused founder gets an
EROSION frame ("this is what quietly takes it apart"). Choose one and commit — a
mixed frame persuades nobody. "Fear of Inaction" is the beat's *function*, not a
mandate to use loss language on a prospect who is not moved by loss.

### BEAT 5 — The Reply Ask (Momentum) — NO money, NO price
**Purpose label:** One low-friction next step — a reply, not a payment
⏱ TIMING: ~2:20
🖥 SCREEN: Clean closing screen.
🎙 SPEAK:
- Give the prospect a reason to reply that benefits HIM, not you. The strongest
  cold-reply motivator is CONTROL over the record about his own business —
  confirming or correcting a stranger's read of his operation is intrinsically
  motivating to a proud founder. Do NOT frame the reply as a favour to you ("I want
  to know if I got it right" makes replying his effort for your benefit — weak).
- Frame the payoff as his: he sets the record straight, and the one thing he
  corrects tells you what he most wants understood.
- **Apply the Reply Friction Rule (Rule 10): the ask must be answerable from a phone,
  one-handed, in under ten words.** Prefer a forced choice over an open question —
  choosing is cheap, composing is expensive, and an unwritten reply is usually a
  reply that needed a laptop. Any reply opens the thread; `p3c` does the rest.
  Approved phrasings (adapt, do not copy verbatim):
  - "Which one did I get wrong — the scheduling read, or the collections read?"
  - "You know the inside, I only see the outside. Which of these two is the real
    problem?"
  - "I'd rather be corrected than flattering — whichever of these you push back on
    tells me what actually matters most in your operation." *(open question — use
    only for High Confidence, where the diagnosis can carry the extra friction)*
- **Close with an explicit permission to dismiss (Rule 5).** One short, sincere
  sentence that hands the decision back: "If I've misread this, tell me and I'll
  leave it there." Do not soften it, qualify it, or follow it with a re-ask. The
  freedom must be real or the effect inverts.
- The ask is a REPLY, never a payment, never a calendar link, never a price.
- Do NOT say: "book a call", "I'd love to chat", "here's my calendar", or name any
  fee. The paid offer comes only later, in `p3c_Messaging_Playbook.md`.
Length: 2–3 sentences, ~45 words.

📌 NOTE: The video ends here. The paid consultation is NOT mentioned in the video.
Once the prospect replies to this video, hand off to `p3c_Messaging_Playbook.md` —
attach this script's finalized `.docx` as an input so the messages quote the exact
stakes number, the exact problems, and the exact proof-of-competence line used
here, rather than re-deriving a version that could drift from what was actually
said on camera.

## CONFIDENCE SIGNAL ADJUSTMENTS

**Authority comes from method, not certainty.** Honesty (Beat 3) builds trust but
can erode the authority that converts to payment later. Resolve that tension by
sourcing authority from the **rigor of the process** ("here is exactly how I'd pin
this down"), never from pretending to be sure about numbers you can't see. Do NOT
close the gap by hedging less — that invites the invented-numbers death the ROI
Integrity rule exists to prevent.

- **High:** Reply ask is direct. Stakes number stated plainly.
- **Medium:** Reply ask invites correction. Stakes number hedged as an estimate.
- **Low:** A Low signal means the diagnosis itself is shaky, and pushing a
  confident stakes claim on thin research is where confident wrongness does the
  most damage. Route gently instead: reply ask is purely curiosity-led, and the
  stakes number is framed as a hypothesis range, not a claim. When in genuine
  doubt at Low confidence, gather more before recording.
