# Execution Playbook — Video, Consultation & Messaging

> **This is the immediate-build scope.** Everything in this document is what
> Abdul needs to execute TODAY to go from prospect → payment.
>
> Three phases, one goal: **get the client to pay for the consultation.**

---

## How to Read This Document

This playbook covers three connected phases:

```
PHASE 1: THE VIDEO          → Earn a reply (FREE — the trust-buyer)
PHASE 2: THE MESSAGING      → Earn a "yes" to the paid consultation
PHASE 3: THE CONSULTATION   → Deliver the diagnosis, earn the demo
```

Each phase has:
- **What it is** — purpose, format, psychology
- **What to do** — step-by-step execution
- **What to say** — exact language, templates, tone
- **What can go wrong** — every scenario and response

# PHASE 1: THE VIDEO

## 1.1 What the Video Is

A 2–3 minute screen-recorded walkthrough of a personalized Decision Safety
Brief (an HTML page). Abdul records himself narrating over the brief, zooming
into one element at a time. Sent as a LinkedIn DM after the warming period.

**Purpose:** Prove you understand the prospect's business better than vendors
they've paid — then STOP. The video is a trailer, not the movie.

**What it is NOT:**
- Not a pitch. Not a demo. Not a sales call.
- Not a generic "we help businesses like yours" message.
- Not a request for a meeting, a call, or money.

**The one thing it must achieve:** Make the prospect think, *"This person
already understands my business — I need the full version of this."*

## 1.2 Prerequisites — What Must Exist Before Recording

Before Abdul records anything, these must be complete:

| Requirement | Source | Purpose |
|-------------|--------|---------|
| Decision Card | `p0a` output | Bottleneck, Confidence Signal, stakes figure |
| Problem Register | `p0b` output | Current problems, future problems, proof ledger |
| Decision Safety Brief (HTML) | `p3a` output | The page Abdul narrates over |
| Warming complete | `p2a` executed | Abdul isn't a stranger — 2-3 LinkedIn comments done |

### The QA Gate (mandatory — do NOT record without clearing this)

The video is sent to a cold CEO. Confident wrongness burns the prospect faster
than no outreach at all. Before recording, confirm ALL of the following:

- [ ] At least **3 problems** are specific, verifiable, and traceable to a
  NAMED public source (their website, a real review, a real post)
- [ ] The primary bottleneck is supported by real evidence, not a single weak
  signal
- [ ] The stakes number has a defensible basis (built from observable data, not
  the unknowns you'll confess in Beat 3)
- [ ] The brief contains NO solution cards, NO ROI dashboard, NO build timeline
- [ ] The CTA is a reply ask — NOT a demo button, calendar link, or price

**If any of these fail:** STOP. Gather more research, or use a generic warm
touch instead. Do not paper over thin research with directional language.

## 1.3 The Video — Beat by Beat

The video follows exactly 5 beats. No beat may be skipped or reordered.

### Beat 1 — Cold Open: Name His Exact Bottleneck
**⏱ 0:00 – 0:20 | ~40 words | Emotion: RECOGNITION**

**What's on screen:** The hero of the brief — the bottleneck headline.

**What to say:** ONE sentence naming the exact operational bottleneck in his
own language. ONE sentence showing you noticed it from how he actually
operates. No introduction, no greeting, no "Hi, I'm Abdul."

**Why this works:** In the first 8 seconds, the prospect hears their own
problem described back to them by a stranger. This is the hook. If they don't
recognize themselves in the first sentence, they stop watching.

**Template shape:**
> "Right now, every [process] at [Company] runs through [bottleneck] — and
> from what I can see in how you [observable signal], that's been the case for
> a while."

**Rules:**
- Start inside the problem. Never start with yourself.
- Use their exact terminology (from their site, posts, job listing).
- No filler: no "So basically", "As you can see", "I'd love to."

---

### Beat 2 — The Homework: Their Real Problems, in Their Words
**⏱ 0:20 – 1:00 | ~90 words | Emotion: RECOGNITION deepens**

**What's on screen:** Scroll slowly down the Current Problem Register table.

**What to say:** Walk 3–4 of their real problems. For each one, state where
you found it (their site, public reviews, posts). Frame it as honest homework
on what's PUBLIC — "anyone who looked closely would see this." Never as deep
surveillance of their internal operations.

**Why this works:** By the third problem, the prospect stops thinking "how
does he know this?" and starts thinking "what else does he know?" This is
curiosity. Curiosity is what earns the reply.

**Template shape:**
> "Looking at [Company]'s [source], three things stood out. First, [problem
> in their words] — I can see that from [where you found it]. Second,
> [problem] — that's visible in [source]. And third, [problem], which shows
> up in [source]. None of this is a guess — it's all in what you've published
> publicly."

**Rules:**
- Cite public sources by name. Never imply you studied private systems.
- Specificity must feel like diligence, not intrusion.
- No opinions yet — just observed facts.

---

### Beat 3 — The Honest Limit: What You Can't See
**⏱ 1:00 – 1:40 | ~90 words | Emotion: TRUST**

**What's on screen:** The Research Transparency table (can-see vs. must-confirm).

**What to say:** Be straight about the boundary of outside research. State
plainly what you CAN see, then name the 2–3 things you CANNOT see yet (the
internal numbers). Say those unknowns are exactly where the real money is,
and the first thing you'd map together.

**Why this works:** This is the most important beat in the entire video. Every
vendor lies. Every agency pretends to know more than they do. When you
voluntarily confess what you can't see, the CEO trusts everything you said
before this moment — because someone who's honest about gaps is probably
honest about findings.

**This beat also creates the bridge to the consultation.** If you can see
everything from outside, there's no reason to pay for a session. The honest
limit IS the reason the paid gate exists.

**Template shape:**
> "Now, what I can see from outside is [observable data]. What I can't see is
> [2-3 internal unknowns — conversion rate, team hours, cost per X]. Those
> numbers are where the real cost of this bottleneck lives. They're the first
> thing I'd want to pin down if we sat down together."

**Critical — The Decoupling Rule:**
The stakes number in Beat 4 must NOT be built from the unknowns you just
confessed here. If Beat 3 says "I can't see your conversion rate," Beat 4
must NOT quote a cost figure that requires the conversion rate. A sharp CEO
will catch: "you said you can't see it, then priced my loss on it."

Build the stakes number from what IS observable. Frame it as conservative.
Close with "the exact figure is what we'd confirm together."

---

### Beat 4 — The Stakes + The Regret Gap
**⏱ 1:40 – 2:20 | ~70 words | Emotion: FEAR OF INACTION**

**What's on screen:** One future-risk card from the brief.

**What to say:** Three moves, in this order:

**Move 1 — The stakes number:**
State the single cost-of-bottleneck figure. Phrased per Confidence Signal:
- High: "You're losing roughly [X]."
- Medium: "My rough estimate is [X] — one of the first things we'd confirm."
- Low: "It could be anywhere from [X] to [Y] — that gap is exactly what the
  session pins down."

**Move 2 — The Regret Gap:**
Name the prospect's OWN stated goal (from their LinkedIn, their website, their
posts). Then show this bottleneck stands between them and that goal — and it
compounds.

> "You've said you want [their stated goal]. Right now, this one gap is the
> single thing capping that — and as you grow, it doesn't stay the same size.
> It doubles."

**Move 3 — Proof of competence (one sentence):**
One sentence showing Dev8X can DELIVER, not just diagnose.

> "This is the kind of operational gap we build systems to close."

**Rules:**
- Pressure the GOAL, never the character. No guilt, no shame.
- The tension is the gap between their ambition and the bottleneck. Self-
  generated, not imposed.
- Keep it short. This beat carries stakes + regret gap + proof — don't cram.

---

### Beat 5 — The Reply Ask
**⏱ 2:20 – 2:40 | ~45 words | Emotion: MOMENTUM**

**What's on screen:** Clean closing screen.

**What to say:** Give the prospect a reason to reply that benefits HIM, not
you. The strongest motivator for a proud founder is CONTROL over the record
about his own business.

**Template shapes (adapt, don't copy verbatim):**
> "I'd rather be corrected than flattering — whichever of these you push back
> on tells me what actually matters most in your operation."

> "You know the inside, I only see the outside — tell me which of these is
> the real problem and which I overweighted."

**Rules:**
- The ask is a REPLY. Never a payment, never a calendar link, never a price.
- Do NOT frame the reply as a favour to you ("I want to know if I got it right"
  makes replying his effort for your benefit — weak).
- Frame the payoff as his: he sets the record straight.
- The paid consultation is NOT mentioned in the video.

## 1.4 The DM Wrapper — What Goes Alongside the Video

The video is only watched if the wrapper message earns the click. This is the
message sent with the video in the LinkedIn DM.

**Requirements:**
- Disarm the cold context up front (who is Abdul, why this video exists)
- Tease the bottleneck without summarizing it
- Light legitimacy signal (Dev8X is a real studio)
- 2–3 sentences. Peer-to-peer. Zero hype.

**Template shape:**
> "We've never spoken, so this is out of the blue — I run a small dev studio
> (Dev8X) and spent an hour on how [Company] handles [process]. I recorded a
> 3-minute read of where I think it's leaking. No pitch — tell me if I'm
> wrong."

**If the video is NOT watched (5-7 days later), send a second wrapper:**
> "Not sure if the video came through — just a 3-minute look at [one specific
> problem]. Thought it was worth flagging. Either way, no pressure."

**Rules:**
- Never more than 2 wrapper attempts. Beyond that, you're spam.
- Never link-spam phrasing. No "Check out our services."
- The wrapper's job is to earn the click, not summarize the video.

## 1.5 Recording Guidelines

- **Burned-in captions** on every spoken line. Many watch on mute.
- **Zoom per element.** Do NOT film a full-page scroll. The brief is dense and
  unreadable as a phone video. Zoom into ONE element at a time: hero stat, one
  problem row, the transparency table.
- **Record at 1280×720 or higher.** Verify smallest on-screen text is
  phone-readable.
- **Calm, unhurried delivery.** ~120 words per minute. A doctor reading an
  X-ray, not a salesman.
- **280–360 total spoken words.** If the beats run long, TRIM. Calm delivery
  that lands > cramming more words.

# PHASE 2: THE MESSAGING SYSTEM

## 2.1 The Linear Path (Happy Path)

When everything goes well, the message sequence is:

```
DM Wrapper + Video
      │
      ▼ (prospect replies)
Message 1: Qualitative question, no pitch
      │
      ▼ (prospect answers)
Message 2: Paid consultation offer (guarantee + stakes + legitimacy + fee)
      │
      ▼ (prospect agrees)
Schedule + payment + pre-call expectations
      │
      ▼
CONSULTATION (Phase 3)
```

## 2.2 Message 1 — Sent Immediately When They Reply

**Purpose:** Reward the reply. Deepen rapport. Qualify. Do NOT pitch.

**What to do:**
- Acknowledge their specific reply (show you read it, not a template)
- Ask ONE qualitative question that builds rapport or qualifies:
  - Which problem stings most?
  - Have they tried to fix it?
  - What would they want to be different?
- Do NOT ask for internal numbers (conversion rate, volume, hours) — capturing
  those is exactly what the paid consultation sells. Asking here gives the
  consultation's value away for free.

**Length:** 2–3 sentences.

**Template shape:**
> "Appreciate the reply — [specific reference to what they said]. Quick
> question: of those three things I flagged, which one is actually costing
> you the most day-to-day?"

---

## 2.3 Message 2 — The Consultation Offer

**Purpose:** Offer the paid session. Sent AFTER they answer Message 1.

**This message must contain, in this order:**

### 1. The Reframe
Frame it as a paid working session that produces a documented diagnosis they
keep. NOT "a sales call." NOT "a discovery call." A paid diagnosis with
standalone value.

> "What I'd normally do next is a working session — about 45 minutes — where
> I walk through everything I've found alongside the numbers only you can
> provide. You walk away with a documented operational diagnosis you keep."

### 2. The Stakes Tie-Back
Reference the Beat 4 number so the fee looks small against what they're losing.

> "Based on what I can see, [bottleneck] is costing you somewhere around
> [stakes number] a year. The session pins that exact figure down."

### 3. The Guarantee (mandatory)
Risk reversal. The client judges "worth it." The diagnosis is kept regardless.

> "If you don't walk away with something worth more than the fee, you don't
> pay. You decide, not me. And the documented diagnosis is yours to keep
> either way."

### 4. The Proof of Competence
One line showing Dev8X can deliver.

> "This is what we do at Dev8X — find the operational gap, document it,
> then build the system that closes it."

### 5. Legitimacy + Payment Reassurance
One factual line that makes paying a near-stranger feel safe.

> "Dev8X is a registered studio — payment runs through a proper invoice,
> not a personal transfer."

### 6. The Price
State it plainly. Use the `[CONSULTATION_FEE]` placeholder until the fee is
defined. Apply fee-anchoring:

- **Not too high** — the leap from free video to paying a stranger is fragile
- **Not too low** — trivially cheap trivialises the offer
- **Always anchor against the stakes** — "a few hundred against the [stakes]
  you're losing every year"

> "The session is [CONSULTATION_FEE]. Against [stakes number], it's a small
> investment to know exactly where you stand."

### 7. The Ask
A single low-friction ask. Reply to confirm, then you send times.

> "If that sounds right, reply and I'll send over a couple of time options."

**Total length:** 6–8 sentences. Confident. No pressure. No discounting.

---

## 2.4 Message 3 — Soft Follow-Up (If No Reply to Message 2)

**Timing:** 3–4 days after Message 2.

**Purpose:** One nudge. Value-first. Easy out.

- Lightly re-surface the core stakes
- Restate the guarantee in half a sentence
- Make saying "not now" easy

**Template shape:**
> "No pressure at all — just wanted to circle back. The [bottleneck] isn't
> going anywhere, and the guarantee still stands (if the session isn't worth
> it, you don't pay). Either way, happy to leave it here if the timing's off."

**Rules:**
- Maximum ONE Message 3. If no reply, STOP. Do not chase.
- Re-engage in 90 days with a fresh industry insight (not about them).

## 2.5 The War-Game Branches

Not every prospect follows the happy path. Here is every scenario with
Abdul's exact response.

### BRANCH A: Correction — "You got it wrong"

Beat 5 explicitly invites correction. Outside-in research will sometimes be
wrong. A mishandled correction burns everything.

**Response rules:**
1. **Thank him genuinely. Do not defend.** Being corrected is a gift.
2. **Absorb and recompute.** If the correction invalidates the stakes number
   or a problem, say so plainly and adjust. Never cling to a figure he just
   refuted.
3. **Turn it into the bridge, honestly:**

> "That's exactly why doing this properly matters — from outside I'll always
> have blind spots. The session is where my guesses get replaced with your
> real numbers."

4. If the correction guts the entire diagnosis ("we don't even do that"):
   - Do NOT push the offer
   - Acknowledge, ask one genuine question, leave the door open
   - A forced offer on a wrong diagnosis destroys credibility

---

### BRANCH B: Silent Watcher — Watched, No Reply

The most common outcome. Many prospects watch, are impressed, and never reply.
Replying costs effort; silence costs nothing.

**Response (ONE touch only, 4-6 days after the video):**

> "No need to reply to the video — one quick question: is [bottleneck]
> actually what's slowing [their goal], or am I off?"

**Rules:**
- Lower the reply bar to near zero — a yes/no answer
- Do NOT re-pitch or repeat the video
- Lead with ONE new observation or a single sharp question
- **Maximum ONE** silent-watcher touch. If still no reply, stop. Further
  messages read as pressure.

---

### BRANCH C: Didn't Watch — Video Not Opened

**Response (5-7 days later):**

> "Not sure if the video came through — it's a 3-minute look at [one specific
> problem I found in your operation]. Thought it was worth flagging. Either
> way, no pressure."

**If still no watch after the second wrapper:**
- Stop. Do not send a third message.
- Re-engage in 90 days with a fresh industry insight (not the original video).

---

### BRANCH D: "Not Interested"

**Response:**

> "Completely fair. If anything changes down the line, the research I did is
> still valid — happy to pick it up anytime. Wishing you well with
> [Company]."

**Rules:**
- Graceful, immediate exit
- No persuasion, no "are you sure?", no counter-argument
- The door stays open. Never burn it.
- Re-engage in 90 days ONLY with a general industry insight (not about them)

---

### BRANCH E: "Too Busy Right Now"

**Response:**

> "Totally understand — timing matters more than anything. When would be a
> better time to revisit? Happy to circle back whenever makes sense for you."

**If they give a time:** Schedule it. Follow up at that exact time.
**If they don't:** 60-day check-in with one industry observation.

---

### BRANCH F: "What's This Going to Cost Me?"

This is a buying signal disguised as an objection. They're asking about cost
because they're considering it.

**Response:**

> "The video and the research I've done are free — no cost to reply. If we end
> up doing a working session together, there's a fee, but it comes with a
> guarantee: if you don't walk away with something worth more than the fee,
> you don't pay. Happy to explain more if you're curious."

**Rules:**
- Don't quote the consultation fee here. Bridge to Message 2.
- Don't be defensive about money. Money is natural.
- The guarantee is the de-risker.

---

### BRANCH G: "Send Me More Info"

**Response:**

> "Happy to — I've attached the research brief I built for [Company]. It's a
> one-page summary of what I found. Take a look and let me know if anything
> stands out."

Send the Decision Safety Brief (p3a HTML) + a one-paragraph summary. Don't
dump everything.

**Follow up in 3-4 days:**
> "Did anything in the brief surprise you, or did it match what you're already
> seeing?"

---

### BRANCH H: "I Already Have a Developer / Agency"

**Response:**

> "That's great — this isn't about replacing anyone. The research I did might
> actually be useful context for whoever handles your tech. Happy to share it
> with them or with you directly."

**Rules:**
- No follow-up unless they re-engage
- You've planted a seed: "this person did free research for me and didn't try
  to steal work from my current provider"
- This often converts 3-6 months later when their current provider disappoints

---

### BRANCH I: "Too Expensive" (After Message 2)

**Response:**

> "I hear you. The way I think about it: the [bottleneck] is costing you
> around [stakes number] every year it stays. The session fee is
> [CONSULTATION_FEE] — and if it doesn't surface something worth more than
> that, you keep the diagnosis and don't pay. The risk is genuinely on my
> side."

**If still no:**

> "Completely understand. The offer and the research stay valid. If the
> numbers change or the bottleneck gets worse, I'm here."

**Rules:**
- Never discount. Discounting signals the original price was inflated.
- Anchor against the stakes number, not against competitors.
- The guarantee is the answer to price objections — it removes risk.

---

### BRANCH J: "I Want a Free Call Instead"

**Response:**

> "I get it — paying a stranger feels like a risk, and I respect that. The
> guarantee exists for exactly that reason: if the session doesn't earn its
> fee, you keep the diagnosis and pay nothing. You decide, not me.
>
> That said, if you'd like a quick 15-minute call just to see if this is
> worth your time, I'm happy to do that first. No diagnosis in that call —
> just a fit check."

**Rules:**
- Hold the line on the paid consultation as the value delivery
- The 15-minute fit check is a concession, not the default
- Only offer it if the prospect is clearly interested but stuck on trust
- The fit check contains NO diagnosis, NO numbers, NO deliverables — it's
  a human conversation to reduce the risk of paying the wrong person

---

### BRANCH K: "Let Me Think About It"

**Response:**

> "Of course — it's a real decision and I respect that. One thing to sit
> with: the [bottleneck] costs roughly [stakes number] every month it stays
> as-is. The session is designed to pin that number down exactly. Either way,
> no rush."

**Follow up in 5-7 days:**

> "Hey — just checking in. Any questions I can answer? Happy to help you
> think it through."

**If still no reply:** Stop. 90-day re-engagement with fresh industry insight.

---

### BRANCH L: "Can You Send References?"

**Response:**

> "Absolutely. [Provide one real reference — a result, a testimonial, a
> previous project outcome]. If you'd like to speak with [previous client]
> directly, I can connect you."

**If you don't have references yet (early-stage):**

> "We're a focused studio — I can walk you through our recent work and the
> results we've delivered. The guarantee also means you're not taking my word
> for it — if the session doesn't deliver, you don't pay."

---

### BRANCH M: "Can You Show Me Examples of Your Work?"

Different from references — they want to SEE deliverables, not hear
testimonials.

**Response:**

> "Happy to. Here's an example of [a relevant project — describe what was
> built, the problem it solved, and one measurable result]. The Decision
> Safety Brief I built for you is itself a sample of how we work — we
> research first, then build."

**If you have a demo portal or project to show:**
Share a link or screenshot. Keep it brief — one example, not a portfolio dump.

**If you're early-stage with no portfolio:**

> "We're selective about what we take on, so the portfolio is growing. The
> brief I sent you is the best example of our process — deep research before
> any build. And the guarantee means you're not risking anything on the
> session."

---

### BRANCH N: "Who Are You?" / "How Did You Find Me?"

Basic identity and discovery questions. Common when the wrapper message
didn't fully disarm the cold context.

**Response (who are you):**

> "I run Dev8X — a small development studio that builds web platforms and
> automation systems for businesses. I spent time researching [Company]
> because [honest reason — I saw your post about X / you came up in my
> network / I was looking at businesses in your space]. The video is the
> result of that research."

**Response (how did you find me):**

> "[Honest, specific answer — through LinkedIn / through your post about X /
> through a connection in [industry]]. I don't do mass outreach — I research
> one business at a time and only reach out if I find something worth
> flagging."

**Rules:**
- Always be honest about the discovery path. Never say "our algorithm found
  you" or anything that sounds automated.
- This is a trust-check. If you pass it, they'll engage. If you dodge it,
  they won't.

---

### BRANCH O: "Actually, Our Real Problem Is [Something Different]"

The prospect engages but redirects to a problem you didn't identify. This is
valuable — it means they're thinking about their business with you.

**Response:**

> "That's really useful to know — from outside, [the problem you identified]
> looked like the main bottleneck, but if [their stated problem] is what's
> actually costing you, that changes the picture. Can you tell me more about
> how it's affecting things day-to-day?"

**Rules:**
- Never defend your original diagnosis. Their view of their business beats
  your outside research.
- Absorb the new problem. Ask one follow-up question.
- If the new problem is solvable with web/automation: bridge to the
  consultation with the updated framing.
- If the new problem is NOT solvable with web/automation (e.g., hiring,
  management, compliance): be honest.

> "That sounds more like a [hiring / operations / management] challenge than
> a systems one. I don't want to force a tech solution where it doesn't fit.
> If there's a web or automation angle to it, I'd love to explore that — but
> if not, I'd rather be straight about it."

---

### BRANCH P: "I Need to Check With My Partner / Board / CTO"

Authority objection. The person you reached is not the sole decision-maker.

**Response:**

> "Of course — that makes sense. Would it help if I put together a short
> summary they can review? One page covering what I found and what the
> session would cover. That way they have context before you discuss it."

**If they say yes:** Send a one-page summary (pull from the Decision Safety
Brief — headline, top 3 problems, stakes number, what the consultation
delivers). Keep it forwardable.

**If they say "I'll just tell them":**

> "Got it. If any questions come up, happy to jump on a quick call with both
> of you — sometimes it's easier to hear it directly."

**Follow up in 5-7 days:**

> "Did you get a chance to discuss with [partner/team]? Any questions I can
> help with?"

**Rules:**
- Never pressure. Multi-stakeholder decisions take time.
- The one-page summary is your proxy — it sells when you're not in the room.
- If the partner says no: graceful exit, door open, 90-day re-engagement.

---

### BRANCH Q: "We're Already In a Contract With Someone Until [Date]"

Timing lock-in. They're interested but legally or financially committed
elsewhere.

**Response:**

> "That's totally fair — no point in doubling up. When does the contract
> wrap up? I'm happy to reconnect closer to that date. The research I've
> done stays valid, and we can pick up right where we left off."

**Rules:**
- Note the contract end date. Set a calendar reminder for 30 days before.
- Re-engage with a fresh insight at that time (not just "your contract is
  ending").
- This is one of the highest-conversion re-engagement segments — they were
  interested, just locked in.

---

### BRANCH R: Prospect Forwards to Someone Else

"My CTO should see this" / "Let me send this to my partner" / "Can I share
this with my team?"

This is a POSITIVE signal — they're bringing others into the conversation.

**Response:**

> "Absolutely — please do. If it's helpful, I can send them a short summary
> that gives context without needing the full video. And if they have
> questions, I'm happy to jump on a quick call with both of you."

**Rules:**
- Always encourage forwarding. This multiplies your reach.
- Offer to engage the new person directly — don't make the original
  prospect your salesperson.
- If the forwarded person reaches out: treat them as a warm lead (they've
  already seen your work through a trusted source).

---

### BRANCH S: Free Consulting Extraction

The prospect keeps asking detailed questions to get free advice without
committing to the paid consultation. They ask things like "So what would you
recommend?" or "What tool should we use for X?" or "Can you explain how
you'd solve [problem]?"

**Detection signals:**
- Multiple detailed questions after Message 1, but no movement toward
  Message 2
- Questions that ask for the consultation's deliverables (solutions,
  architecture, recommendations)
- "Just one more question" pattern

**Response (gentle redirect):**

> "Great question — that's exactly the kind of thing the session is built to
> answer properly. I could give you a quick take here, but the honest answer
> is it depends on your numbers, and I'd rather give you the right answer
> than a fast one. That's what the session is for."

**Rules:**
- Never be rude or accusatory. Most people don't realize they're doing it.
- Give enough to show competence, withhold enough to justify the paid gate.
- The line: factual observations about their problem = free. Specific
  recommendations, solutions, or architecture = paid.
- If it continues after the redirect: "I want to give you a proper answer,
  not a corridor one. The session is where I can do that." Then stop
  answering substantive questions.

## 2.6 The LinkedIn Connection Request

There's a gap between warming (comments) and video (DM) that many overlook:
the connection request itself.

**When to send:** After Comment 2 or 3 has received engagement (a like, a
reply, or they've viewed your profile).

**Connection request message (keep it short — LinkedIn limits to 300 chars):**

> "Hey [Name] — been following your posts on [topic]. I run a dev studio
> that works in [their space]. Would be good to connect."

**Rules:**
- No pitch in the connection request. Zero.
- No "I noticed you could use help with..." — this triggers sales-spam
  pattern recognition.
- The connection request is a handshake, not a sales letter.
- Wait at least 24-48 hours after connection is accepted before sending
  the video.

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|

**The universal rule:** If continuing feels like chasing, stop. Pressure
destroys the relationship for any future contact. Dev8X's edge is depth and
trust — not persistence and volume.

# PHASE 3: THE CONSULTATION

## 3.1 What the Consultation Is

A live, paid, 45–60 minute video call (Zoom/Meet) where Abdul sits down with
the prospect and converts the outside-in hypothesis into an inside-out
confirmed diagnosis, using the client's own real numbers.

**What the client pays for:** A documented Operational Diagnosis they keep.
It's R&D on their business, not a sales call.

**What makes it worth the fee:** The first video showed problems from the
outside. The consultation gets the REAL numbers — the ones you couldn't see.
Those numbers upgrade every estimate into a fact. The client walks away
knowing exactly what their bottleneck is costing them.

## 3.2 How the Consultation Gets Offered

The consultation is ONLY offered in Message 2, AFTER dialogue exists. Never
in the video. Never in the DM wrapper. Never as the first thing you say.

**The sequence:**
1. Video earns a reply
2. Message 1 builds rapport + qualifies
3. Message 2 offers the consultation with guarantee + stakes + legitimacy

**If the prospect asks about the consultation before Message 2** (e.g., in
their initial reply): skip Message 1 and go directly to Message 2. Don't
create unnecessary friction when they're already moving forward.

## 3.3 Pre-Consultation Logistics

### Payment Timing

Payment is collected BEFORE the consultation, not after. The guarantee
protects them (full refund if not worth it), so pre-payment is not a risk
for the client — it's a commitment signal.

**Process:**
1. Prospect agrees to Message 2
2. Abdul sends invoice (professional, from Dev8X, not personal)
3. Payment confirmed
4. Calendar invite sent with time + link
5. Pre-call message sent (see below)
6. Consultation happens

**Invoice format:** Professional, branded, from Dev8X. Include:
- Session description: "Operational Diagnosis — [Company Name]"
- Fee: [CONSULTATION_FEE]
- Payment terms: due before session
- Guarantee: stated on the invoice
- Dev8X registration/business details

### Pre-Call Confirmation Message (sent 24-48 hours before)

After payment is confirmed and the call is scheduled, send:

> "Looking forward to our session on [day] at [time]. Here's what to expect:
>
> — We'll go through the problems I flagged and confirm which ones hit
>   hardest.
> — I'll ask about a few numbers only you can provide — things like volume,
>   time spent, conversion.
> — We'll calculate the real cost of the bottleneck together, on screen.
> — You'll walk away with a documented diagnosis you keep.
>
> No prep needed on your end — just bring what you know about your
> operations. Talk soon."

**Why this matters:** It reduces no-shows, sets expectations, and reminds
them this is a working session — not a pitch.

### No-Show Protocol

| Situation | Response | Timing |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| Doesn't join within 10 min | Send a DM: "Hey — we were set for [time]. Still available if you can join in the next few minutes." | 10 min after scheduled start |
| Doesn't join within 20 min | End the wait. Send: "Looks like today didn't work out. No worries — happy to reschedule. Let me know what works." | 20 min after |
| First no-show | Offer one reschedule, no penalty | Same day |
| Second no-show | "I want to make sure the timing works for you. When you're ready to lock in a time, let me know and we'll set it up." | After second miss |
| Third no-show | Stop. Do not chase. If they reach out later, reschedule with payment re-confirmed. | — |

### Rescheduling Protocol

- **First reschedule:** No friction. "Of course — let me know what works better."
- **Second reschedule:** Gentle check: "Happy to move it — want to make sure
  we find a time that actually holds."
- **Third reschedule:** "I think the timing might not be right just now. The
  research stays valid — whenever you're ready, we pick it up. No pressure."
  Stop scheduling. Ball is in their court.

**Rule:** After 2 reschedules, do not chase. If they come back, the session
happens. If they don't, 90-day re-engagement.

## 3.4 Pre-Call Setup

Before the consultation, ensure:

- [ ] Decision Safety Brief open in a tab (the problems the client saw)
- [ ] Live Capture Sheet (Part B) open and screen-shareable
- [ ] Discovery Questions reviewed (from Decision Card §9 + Problem Register)
- [ ] Cost formula pre-loaded with blanks
- [ ] Recording permission question ready
- [ ] Invoice/payment confirmed

## 3.4 The Call — Section by Section

### Section 0 — Setup & Frame (0:00, ~3 min)

**Goal:** Reset expectation — this is a working session producing a kept
deliverable.

**What to say:**
- Permission to record / share notes
- Confirm time (45-60 min)
- Frame: "You'll leave with a documented diagnosis. I'll ask things only you
  can answer, then show where it points."

---

### Section 1 — Confirm the Problems (3:00, ~8 min)

**Goal:** Turn each confirmed problem into a micro-commitment.

**What's on screen:** The Current Problem Register from the brief.

**For each top 3-4 problems:**
- "Last time I flagged [problem in their words] — is that right? What did I
  get wrong?"
- Listen for confirmation or correction
- Capture corrections verbatim — their wording wins over yours
- Every confirmed problem is a building block for the cost calculation later

---

### Section 2 — Capture the Real Numbers (11:00, ~18 min)

**Goal:** Get the L1 numbers the first video couldn't see. THIS IS THE HEART
OF THE CALL.

**Group the Discovery Questions into these themes, in order:**
1. **Volume** — how many [units/clients/transactions] per [period]?
2. **Time/Effort** — how long does [process] take? Who does it?
3. **Conversion/Loss** — what percentage of [X] becomes [Y]? Where do you
   lose people?
4. **Cost/Value** — what's each [unit] worth? What do you pay for [process]?

**For each question:**
- Ask conversationally. One question at a time.
- Listen for the number. Note which L2 estimate it upgrades to L1.
- Capture in the Live Capture Sheet, on screen, in front of them.
- If they don't know a number, capture it as "to confirm" — do not pressure.

**Rules:**
- Never stack three questions in one breath. Ask, then listen.
- Never lead the witness ("so it's about 40, right?"). Ask neutrally.
- The client should talk ~60% of the call. Questions and capture, not
  monologue.

---

### Section 3 — Quantify the Cost, Live (29:00, ~8 min)

**Goal:** Make the bottleneck concrete and expensive — in THEIR numbers.

**What's on screen:** The Live Capture Sheet cost block.

**What to do:** Build the cost-of-bottleneck calculation on screen from the
numbers just captured:

```
[lost units/month] × [conversion rate] × [value per unit] × 12 = [annual cost]
```

**What to say after the number appears:**
> "Does that match your gut, or is it higher than you expected?"

**Rules:**
- This is now THEIR number, not your estimate. Let it land.
- Don't rush past it. Silence after the number is powerful.
- This number will anchor every future conversation (demo, proposal, pricing).

---

### Section 4 — Solution Direction (37:00, ~10 min)

**Goal:** First Relief. Show the SHAPE of the fix — direction only.

**What's on screen:** A simple architecture/flow diagram (boxes and arrows).
NOT software.

**For each top problem:**
- Name the conceptual capability that closes it (e.g., "an after-hours
  capture flow", "an automated recall system", "a single dashboard")
- Name the manual operation it removes
- Draw from the Manual Operations Mapping in the Problem Register

**HOLD-BACK ENFORCEMENT:**
No portals. No screens. No build cost. If the client pushes ("can I see
it?"), the approved line is:

> "That's exactly the demo — the next step is I build this working, around
> your operations, and walk you through it."

---

### Section 5 — Leave-Behind Handoff (47:00, ~3 min)

**Goal:** Close the value loop — confirm what they're getting for the fee.

**What to say:**
> "Within [N] days, you'll receive the written Operational Diagnosis —
> everything we confirmed today: the problems, your numbers, the cost
> calculation, and the direction of the fix. That's yours to keep."

---

### Section 6 — Tee Up the Demo + Stakeholders (50:00, ~7 min)

**Goal:** Make the paid demo the obvious next step.

**What to say:**
> "The next step, if you want to take it, is I build the direction we just
> sketched into a working system around your operations and walk you through
> it."

**Critical question:**
> "Who else should be in the room when I walk through the working version?"

Capture names and roles. This identifies all decision-makers early — not at
the proposal stage when it's too late.

**Rules:**
- Do NOT quote a build price. The demo is the next gate.
- The demo fee is separate from the build pricing.
- If they ask about build cost: "That comes after the demo — once you've
  seen it working, we scope and price it together."

## 3.5 The Leave-Behind — Operational Diagnosis

Delivered within [N] days after the call. Client-facing, clean, professional.
No internal framework language.

**Sections:**
1. **The Bottleneck** — one paragraph, their confirmed primary problem
2. **What We Confirmed Together** — confirmed problems, in their words
3. **Your Numbers** — captured metrics in a clean table (labelled "from your
   own figures")
4. **What This Is Costing You** — annual cost calculation, shown plainly
5. **The Direction of the Fix** — conceptual solution direction + manual
   operations each removes. NO working solution. NO cost.
6. **Recommended Next Step** — the working demo

## 3.6 Post-Call System Step

After the call:
1. Feed captured L1 numbers back into the Decision Card §8
2. Re-assess the Confidence Signal (typically upgrades toward High)
3. This strengthens the demo and proposal automatically

## 3.7 Post-Consultation Follow-Up Sequence

The call covered the demo tee-up in Section 6, but a written follow-up
reinforces it.

### Same-day email (within 2 hours of the call ending)

> "Thanks for the session today — it was a productive one. A few things:
>
> 1. The written Operational Diagnosis will be in your inbox within [N] days.
> 2. The cost figure we landed on — [annual cost from Section 3] — is
>    probably the most important number that came out of today.
> 3. As we discussed, the next step is building the direction we sketched
>    into a working system and walking you through it. I'll include the
>    details in the diagnosis document.
>
> Thanks again for your time."

### Follow-up when the diagnosis is delivered (N days later)

> "The Operational Diagnosis is attached. Everything we confirmed together
> is in there — your numbers, the cost calculation, and the direction.
>
> If you want to see it working, let me know and I'll outline what the
> demo looks like."

### If no response to the diagnosis (7 days after delivery)

> "Just checking in — did the diagnosis match what you expected? Any
> questions on what we found?"

### If still no response (14 days after delivery)

> "No pressure at all. The diagnosis is yours to keep. If the bottleneck
> comes back up or you want to explore the demo, I'm here."

**After this:** Stop. 90-day re-engagement with a fresh insight.

# APPENDIX A: CONSULTATION CONTINGENCIES

## A.1 The Problem Is Not Solvable With Web/Automation

Sometimes the consultation reveals that the real bottleneck is hiring,
management, training, or a process that doesn't benefit from technology.

**What to do:**
- Be honest immediately. Never force a tech solution where it doesn't fit.
- Say: "Based on what you've told me, this looks more like a [hiring /
  process / management] challenge than a systems one. I could build
  something, but I don't think it would solve the root cause."
- If there IS a partial tech angle: "The core issue is [non-tech], but
  there's a piece of this — [specific workflow] — where a system could
  help. Want me to focus on that?"
- If there's NO tech angle: deliver the diagnosis as-is. It's still
  valuable. They paid for clarity, not for a sales pitch.

**This honesty is rare and memorable.** A consultant who says "I can't help
you here" earns more trust than one who forces a solution. They'll come
back — or refer someone who does need tech.

## A.2 The Call Runs Over Time

The discovery section (Section 2) often runs long because the client starts
talking about their business in depth. This is GOOD — it means they trust
you and are engaged.

**Rules:**
- At the 45-minute mark, check: "We're at 45 minutes — I want to respect
  your time. We have the numbers, but I still want to show you the
  direction. Can we take 10 more minutes?"
- Never go over 75 minutes. If needed, split into two sessions.
- Prioritize: numbers + cost calculation > solution direction. If time is
  tight, cover the cost live and send the solution direction in the leave-
  behind.

## A.3 Difficult or Dismissive Client

Rare, but possible: the client challenges everything, is dismissive, or
appears disengaged.

**Rules:**
- Stay calm and diagnostic. Never match their energy.
- Ask: "What would make this session most valuable for you?" — let them
  redirect.
- If they're dismissive of the research: "What did I miss? I'd rather
  fix it now than deliver something that doesn't match."
- If genuinely hostile: "I think we might not be the right fit for each
  other, and that's completely fine. I'll send the diagnosis based on
  what we discussed, and the guarantee applies — you decide if it was
  worth it."
- Never argue. Never get defensive. The guarantee is your shield.

## A.4 Client Brings Extra People to the Call

Sometimes the client shows up with their CTO, partner, or team member
unannounced.

**Rules:**
- Welcome it. More stakeholders = higher chance of closing the demo.
- At the start: "Great to have [name] here. Let me quickly recap what
  we've covered so everyone has context." (30 seconds max)
- Adjust the discovery to include the new person: "[Name], from your
  side, does [problem] match what you're seeing?"
- Capture their role for the stakeholder map.

## A.5 Client Asks for the Build Price During the Call

Common, especially after the cost calculation lands.

**Approved response:**

> "I don't want to give you a number without seeing it working first.
> The demo is the next step — I build the direction we sketched into a
> working system, and once you've seen it, we scope and price it
> together. That way the price is based on something real, not a guess."

**Rules:**
- Never quote a build price in the consultation. Ever.
- The demo is the next paid gate. Pricing comes after.
- If they push hard: "I want the price to be honest. Right now it would
  be a range with too many unknowns. The demo closes those unknowns."

## A.6 Consultation Recording

If the client grants recording permission:
- Record the session (Zoom/Meet recording)
- Use the recording to produce an accurate Operational Diagnosis
- Do NOT share the recording with the client unless they ask
- The recording is an internal tool — it ensures the diagnosis is precise
- If the client asks for the recording: share it. No reason to withhold.

# APPENDIX B: THE 15-MINUTE FIT CHECK

Offered only in Branch J (prospect wants a free call instead of paying).
This is NOT the consultation. It contains NO diagnosis, NO numbers, NO
deliverables.

**Purpose:** Reduce the trust gap. Let them meet Abdul, hear his voice,
assess whether he's credible — so paying a stranger feels safer.

**Structure (15 min max):**

| Time | What Happens |
|------|-------------|
| 0:00 | "Thanks for taking the time. This is just a quick fit check — not a diagnosis. I want to make sure the session would actually be useful for you before you invest in it." |
| 1:00 | "Tell me a bit about [Company] — what's the main thing keeping you up at night operationally?" (Let them talk. Listen.) |
| 5:00 | "Based on what I researched, the thing I'd want to dig into most is [primary bottleneck]. Does that match?" |
| 8:00 | "The session would go deep on that — get your real numbers, calculate what it's costing, and map the direction of a fix. You keep the documented diagnosis regardless." |
| 10:00 | "Any questions about how it works?" |
| 13:00 | "If it sounds worthwhile, I'll send over the details. If not, no pressure at all." |
| 15:00 | End. |

**Rules:**
- NEVER give diagnosis in the fit check. No numbers, no solutions, no
  architecture.
- If they start asking substantive questions: "That's exactly what the
  session is built for — I want to give you a proper answer, not a
  corridor one."
- Maximum 15 minutes. If the call goes longer, you're giving a free
  consultation.
