This book is designed for anyone who wants to examine their own biases and behaviors with a deeper critical lens in order to take action, make change, and engage positively in the fight for racial equality. The author argues that a massive re-education effort is needed to address stereotypes that have proven harmful — and too often deadly — to the Black community.

## O1 — Biography

url: https://omekongo.com/2014/01/biography/
type: article
date: January 2014
meta: none

Omékongo Dibinga is an UPstander, not a bystander. His life's mission is to inspire people across the globe to take a stand when they witness injustice. He is a youth motivational speaker, trilingual poet, CNN contributor, TV talk show host, and rapper. His Urban Music Award-winning work has been described by Nikki Giovanni as "outstanding, exciting, and new while being very old." His book "From the Limbs of My Poetree" was described by Essence Magazine as a remarkable and insightful collection of poetry. He was one of 5 international recipients out of 750,000 to win the first-ever "CNN iReport Spirit Award" and has received over 1,000,000 views on CNN.com.

His music and writings have appeared alongside artists such as Sheryl Crow, Angelina Jolie, Norah Jones, Damien Rice, Angelique Kidjo, Don Cheadle, and Mos Def. He has shared the stage with Wyclef Jean, OutKast, Sonia Sanchez, Dennis Brutus, and The Last Poets. He has shared his work in 19 countries on 3 continents. He hosts the talk show "Real Talk," which addresses issues facing youth today. His work has appeared in O Magazine and on CNN, BET, BBC, NPR, Music Choice, and Voice of America, reaching millions of homes in over 150 countries. He has written songs for movies and organizations including NASA and the Enough Project.

Omékongo has studied at Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Georgetown, Morehouse, and The Fletcher School, where he received his M.A. in Law & Diplomacy. He is a Ph.D. candidate in International Education Policy at the University of Maryland. He worked with the Southern Poverty Law Center's "Teaching Diverse Students Initiative" and as a Teaching Assistant to Dr. Michael Eric Dyson at Georgetown. He has lectured at TEDx, Harvard, Russell Simmons' Hip-Hop Summit, and the Nuyorican Poets Café. His 1,000,000 Youth Campaign has directly impacted over 75,000 youth globally.

His publishing company, Free Your Mind Publishing (a subdivision of UPstander International), has produced 7 fusion music and motivational CDs, 6 books, and one independent DVD. His motivational book "G.R.O.W. Towards Your Greatness! 10 Steps to Living Your Best Life" has received praise from speakers such as Willie Jolley.

## O9 — 1,000,000 Youth Campaign

url: https://omekongo.com/2014/01/1000000-youth-campaign/
type: article
date: January 2014
meta: none

### The Problem

Youth are bombarded by unattainable media images while facing a shortage of support systems. Community programs are being defunded, school counselors are overworked, and parents are stretched thin. The result is increased rates of suicide, crime, gang activity, dropout, teen sexual promiscuity, and drug abuse.

### The Campaign

The 1,000,000 Youth Campaign exists to speak positivity into the lives of youth. Through the campaign, participants learn the "Great 8" formula for personal growth, including how to:

- Overcome obstacles
- View suicide as a permanent solution to a temporary problem
- Understand the continued relevance of education
- Set goals in pursuit of their dreams
- Remove negative influences from their lives
- Attract people and circumstances that advance their goals
- Respect the differences of others
- Be upstanders, not bystanders, in the face of bullying

### The Method

Through spoken word, hip-hop, personal stories, affirmations, and dialogue, Omékongo empowers audiences to live their greatest life. Intense breakout sessions develop self-esteem and respect for diverse backgrounds.

### Navy Yard Shooting Should Make You Value Life More

Reflection on the fragility of life amid tragedies and violence. Calls readers to celebrate every day as a blessing and to use kind actions to potentially prevent future atrocities.

### The Future of Youth: When Your Child Gets Called a Monkey

Personal essay on the experience of racism as a father after Omékongo's daughter (then age 5) was called a monkey by classmates. Traces his own experiences of racism in Boston, the racial development theory of William Cross, and his commitment to raising daughters with pride in their heritage. Discusses the importance of representation in media, including the impact of "The Princess & The Frog" on his daughters.

### Jay-Z Is More of an Activist Than You Think

Defense of Jay-Z as a socially responsible activist, examining: his UN partnership for the Water for Life project building 1,000 play pumps in Africa; the Shawn Carter Foundation's scholarship fund and Hurricane Sandy charity work; and his campaign efforts to elect and re-elect President Obama. Argues critics overlook Jay-Z's activism by focusing only on his commercial music.

Reflection on Oprah's "Life Class" episodes on absentee fatherhood. Challenges readers to examine their own presence in the lives of those around them — avoiding phone distraction, avoiding text-only communication, actively celebrating one's partner.

Written in response to the George Zimmerman acquittal. Argues that while marches are valid, lasting change requires increasing efforts to save youth from violence, demanding more positive self-representation in media and culture, and holding the community accountable. Calls for BUYcotts alongside boycotts to fund media that showcases Black people in a positive light.

Personal essay on the priorities of Black America after the Zimmerman trial. Argues the community must be as vigilant about Black-on-Black violence as it is about interracial violence, and challenges cultural products and behaviors that undermine Black community self-respect.

### It's Bigger Than Gun Control

CNN.com commentary arguing that beyond gun control policy, the U.S. must address its broader addiction to violent means of expression.

### Zig Ziglar: Simply the Best

CNN.com tribute to Zig Ziglar, praising him as a messenger whose lessons about business, family, and friends remain inspiring.

### White America Isn't Dead!

CNN.com commentary arguing it is a mistake to declare the White American electorate dead. Contends that values and beliefs of Americans broadly — not just racial demographics — should be the primary lens for political analysis.

## O18 — The Future of Youth: 3 Things I Learned from Youth in Mali

url: https://omekongo.com/2014/01/the-future-of-youth-3-things-i-learned-from-youth-in-mali/
type: article
date: January 2014
meta: none

UPstander International partnered with the U.S. State Department to provide leadership training for youth workers in Mali, West Africa — a country in crisis due to conflict with northern Islamic extremists. All presentations were delivered in French. Three key lessons learned:

1. **Youth everywhere want genuine inspiration.** Regardless of language, youth respond to speakers they believe genuinely care about them.
2. **Malian youth place serious value on education.** Most African youth see education as the primary path to success, unlike some U.S. youth who are drawn by media myths of overnight fame.
3. **Youth everywhere respond to uplifting music.** The entertainment industry underestimates youth appetite for positive music. In 19 countries visited, students consistently expressed surprise that uplifting music could even exist.

Omékongo trained over 30 organizational leaders in the evenings and delivered "G.R.O.W. Towards Your Greatness!" to middle schools, high schools, and universities during the day.

## O19 — A Case for Jay-Z, Hip-Hop's Finest MC

url: https://omekongo.com/2014/01/a-case-for-jay-z-hip-hops-finest-mc-repost-wlinks/
type: article
date: January 2014
meta: none

An intellectual defense of Jay-Z as the greatest hip-hop lyricist, written as a rebuttal to an argument for Nas. Omékongo, whose doctoral dissertation is an intellectual biography of Jay-Z, traces his own journey from Jay-Z skeptic to admirer after engaging with the "Black Album" (2003). Key arguments:

- Jay-Z's commercial framing obscures genuine political consciousness and social commentary present throughout his catalog.
- His use of double, triple, and quadruple entendres is unmatched; a single lyric can operate simultaneously as street narrative, racial critique, and cultural commentary.
- Jay-Z's charitable work includes UN water pump projects in Africa, the Shawn Carter Foundation, Hurricane Sandy relief, and Obama campaign fundraising.
- His decision to make commercially accessible music was deliberate and strategic: he believed he could not help the poor while being one of them.

Ultimately argues both Nas and Jay-Z are needed: Nas for local consciousness, Jay-Z for international platform and reach.
