Reopst Post 9
Why I don't like the phrase "Keep the human in the loop"?

Because it suggests that the roles of humans and AI are equal.

That's far from being true (at least for now).

In most of today's working processes, humans are still the ones who understand the broader context, who need to perform and are, essentially, accountable for the results of their work.

AI, on the other hand, is still only a (really useful and powerful) tool that helps us with our work.

That's why I prefer the view that puts the human at the centre. We're controlling the work, we're making the decisions, and we're accountable for our work.

And that's why I giggle every time I encounter an AI-based solution that's supposed to do the work instead of us.

We're not there yet. Feel free to convince me otherwise.

Lost in between the LLM wars and Canva's cringe rap performance, you might have missed a crucial research paper by Google: "𝗧𝗼𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗥𝗲𝘀𝗽𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗶𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗗𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗹𝗼𝗽𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝗚𝗲𝗻𝗲𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗔𝗜 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗘𝗱𝘂𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻: 𝗔𝗻 𝗘𝘃𝗮𝗹𝘂𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻-𝗗𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗲𝗻 𝗔𝗽𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗮𝗰𝗵."

This paper accompanied the release of a family of language models fine-tuned for learning, suitably called 𝗟𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗟𝗠. The paper highlights the biggest flaw of current AI models for educational purposes: 𝙇𝙇𝙈𝙨 𝙖𝙧𝙚 𝙩𝙧𝙖𝙞𝙣𝙚𝙙 𝙩𝙤 𝙗𝙚 "𝙝𝙚𝙡𝙥𝙛𝙪𝙡" 𝙧𝙖𝙩𝙝𝙚𝙧 𝙩𝙝𝙖𝙣 𝙚𝙭𝙥𝙡𝙞𝙘𝙞𝙩𝙡𝙮 𝙤𝙥𝙩𝙞𝙢𝙞𝙨𝙚𝙙 𝙛𝙤𝙧 𝙩𝙚𝙖𝙘𝙝𝙞𝙣𝙜.

The paper's findings focus on their first model, 𝗟𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗟𝗠-𝗧𝘂𝘁𝗼𝗿 (the fine-tuned model). It significantly outperformed Gemini 1.0 (the base model) by:
💡 Staying on topic better
💡Guiding users towards the answer
💡Promoting active engagement
💡Adapting to the learner's level

However, LearnLM-Tutor is also perceived as less friendly and encouraging than Gemini 1.0. Soon enough, Google will fine-tune their next generation models, which will probably take another leap forward.

Kudos to Google for focusing on two crucial shortcomings in education:
1️⃣ Lack of high quality training data
2️⃣ Lack of robust and reproducible assessment frameworks

If you're leveraging existing LLMs for educational purposes, you already know there's much to be desired for.

Building learning applications on top of better foundation models will increase the impact of learning technologies in ways we couldn't have dreamt of a couple of years ago. 💪
