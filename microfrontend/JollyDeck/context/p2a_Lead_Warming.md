## Lead Warming Plan — Igor Čenar / JollyDeck

### Analysis

#### Target CEO — Key Themes

- **Lean over bloated** — Igor argues publicly and repeatedly that large platforms with too many features produce lower user satisfaction and burden daily operations. His core belief: software should do exactly what you need, nothing more. He calls over-featured SaaS "software obesity."
- **Human at the centre, not AI** — He is skeptical of AI systems that claim to replace human judgment. His view: AI is a powerful tool, humans remain accountable and in control. He finds "keep the human in the loop" language inadequate — he wants humans at the centre, not a co-pilot.
- **The original SaaS promise is being broken** — He sees per-user pricing inflation as a betrayal of what SaaS was supposed to be: affordable, simple, no lock-in. JollyDeck explicitly positions itself as still honouring that original promise.
- **Reliability as a quiet competitive edge** — He posted about 100% uptime during a major platform outage. His belief: reliability is not a feature, it is an expectation. He runs on dedicated infrastructure specifically to own this.
- **Learning from four generations of mistakes** — He openly documents JollyDeck's failed product generations (complex UX, missing editor controls, premature chatbot). He values honest retrospective thinking over polished founder narratives.
- **Speed and simplicity as design philosophy** — Not just for the product. For the company. For decision-making. He is drawn to tools, ideas, and people who remove friction rather than add it.

#### Target CEO — Likely Pain Points

- **Growing the platform with a 5-person team** — JollyDeck is shipping features aggressively (multiple per month) with a tiny team. Prioritisation, technical debt, and bandwidth are constant constraints.
- **LinkedIn reach is not matching product quality** — 551 followers after 10+ years, with 200+ enterprise clients including Vodafone and Deutsche Bahn. The audience is far smaller than the product's credibility warrants. Igor is posting consistently but the distribution isn't breaking through.
- **No outbound sales function** — Everything is inbound. Free plan → self-serve upgrade. Enterprise → direct contact. There is no proactive pipeline. For a CEO selling to enterprise L&D teams, this is a significant ceiling.
- **Translation and localisation bottleneck** — JollyDeck supports 14 languages and is adding more. Every product update triggers a translation cycle. They hire freelancers per language via Upwork. This is slow and hard to scale.
- **No CRM or marketing automation** — Free-to-paid nurturing, enterprise follow-up, and renewal management appear to be handled manually. For a SaaS business at this stage, that is a meaningful operational gap.

#### Dev8X — Relevant Overlap

- **Abdul runs a tech company building software products** — Igor has spent four product generations learning how to build software that people actually use. Abdul operates in the same mental world: the tension between what users ask for and what they actually need.
- **Both operate lean** — Dev8X, as a tech company, faces the same founder reality Igor describes: small team, high product scope, decisions made fast. The "software obesity" post is something Abdul can respond to from firsthand experience, not borrowed opinion.
- **The SaaS pricing shift affects Dev8X too** — If Abdul's company uses SaaS tools — or builds them — the post about per-user pricing inflation and the original SaaS promise is directly relevant. This is not a tangential topic.
- **AI tools used in production, not as hype** — Igor is pragmatic about LLMs (references Google DeepMind research, uses AI in his own work, but is sceptical of AI-replaces-human framing). Abdul as a tech CEO has daily contact with the same tension. This shared nuance is rare and credible common ground.
- **Habit and switching cost in software** — Igor wrote about why people cling to old software. This is a real problem for anyone selling B2B software or building products that ask users to change a workflow. Abdul lives this problem.

#### Shared Ground

Both Igor and Abdul are founder-CEOs of tech companies who have built software products through multiple iterations, made real mistakes, and arrived at the same operating conviction: the best software does less, more reliably, and treats the user as someone who needs to get work done — not someone who needs to be impressed. That is not a surface similarity. It is the same founding thesis reached independently.
