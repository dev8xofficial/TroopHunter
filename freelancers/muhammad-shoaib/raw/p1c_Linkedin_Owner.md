Moving fast is important.

But moving fast without structure can quietly slow the business down later.

McKinsey reports that 10-20% of new-product technology budget can be diverted to resolving technical debt.

For some teams, the cost is even higher.

This shows up as slower releases, fragile integrations, expensive changes, and delayed product ideas.

Technical debt is not just a developer problem.

It becomes a business problem when every new feature takes longer than expected.

The goal is not perfect code.

The goal is sustainable progress.

Build for today’s launch.
Plan for tomorrow’s scale.

Where have you seen a shortcut become expensive later?

AI is becoming part of everyday software development.

According to the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, 84% of respondents are already using or planning to use AI tools in their development process.

But there is another side to the story:

46% of developers actively distrust the accuracy of AI-generated output.
Only 3% say they highly trust it.

That does not mean AI is ineffective.

It means businesses need to use it carefully.

AI can help teams move faster with repetitive tasks, early drafts, debugging support, and documentation.

But strong products still require code review, testing, technical ownership, and human judgment.

The goal is not to replace the engineering discipline.

It is to make good engineering teams more effective.

How is your team using AI without compromising product quality?
