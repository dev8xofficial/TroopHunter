## CONTEXT

**The sender:** Abdul — frontend developer, 6 years with React, Next.js, TypeScript.
Also full-stack. Looking for overflow and task-level work.

**Why Path A was chosen:** The strategy prompt selected this path because the prospect
is either too busy for a longer message, replied briefly to the warming comments, or
the research does not contain enough detail for a credible personalized observation in
a longer format. A short opener that invites a reply is the right move.

**The goal:** One reply. That is all. Not an agreement to work, not a call booked,
not a pitch accepted. One reply that keeps the conversation alive.

---

## RULES

### Message Rules

**Length:** Under 60 words. Hard limit. Count before finalising.

**Structure — three beats in sequence:**

1. **Hook (1 sentence):** Reference something specific from their profile, posts, or
   work history. This is what proves this is not a mass message. It must be concrete —
   a project type they work on, a tool they use, a problem they posted about, a pattern
   in their work history. Not a compliment. An observation.

2. **Bridge (1 sentence, optional):** One sentence connecting the hook to something
   Abdul has relevant experience with — or skip entirely if the hook is strong enough
   to stand alone. If including, make it factual and brief. No pitch language.

3. **Question (1 sentence):** One direct, easy-to-answer question that follows
   naturally from the hook. The answer should require no more than two sentences from
   the prospect. Do not ask "are you open to collaborating?" — ask something specific
   about their work that any developer would be comfortable answering.

**What not to do:**
- Do not introduce Abdul by name, role, company, or background.
- Do not mention any company.
- Do not reference the portfolio link.
- Do not ask for a call, a meeting, or 15 minutes of their time.
- Do not use the prospect's first name in the message.
- Do not open with "Hey", "Hi", "Hope this finds you well", or any greeting.
- Do not use exclamation marks.
- Do not end with "Let me know if you're interested" or any pressure phrase.

**Tone:** The same tone established in the warming comments — direct, peer-level,
zero pressure. Read the lead warming plan to match the register already set.

**Test before finalising:**
- Would a stranger receiving this think it was written specifically for them? If not, rewrite.
- Could this be sent to a different developer without changing it? If yes, rewrite.
- Does the question have an easy, natural answer? If not, simplify it.
- Read it aloud. If it sounds scripted, rewrite it.
