About
I’ve been a working programmer since 1995 where I got my start at Netpaths as a jr. Web Developer. For a large portion of my career, I focused on Flash Game Development and Object Oriented Programming. But as Flash began it's decent I jumped on to Open Source bandwagon with AngularJS and give JavaScript another shot. I later made the migration to React/Redux and began to adopt Functional Programming paradigms. I don’t know how I suffered with OOP for so long! Currently working at FabFitFun as a full stack engineer using React/Redux, Java/DropWizard, and MySql. In my spare time, I’m always tinkering with new tech libraries, frameworks, and languages. Developing web applications in this day and age couldn’t be more enjoyable! I'm currently working towards my Masters of Science in Computer Science from Georgia Tech. I’ve been in this game a long time now. Since ~1997 when I joined my high school’s IT support team. Along the way I’ve met many people and I was fortunate to capture many words of wisdom.

Here’s one that has come to the surface. “Learn the hard stuff that needs to get done but no one wants to do” This was told to me by the founding engineer at a Fintech company I worked at. 

In 1998 I learned to hack my WebTV and that lead me to make websites and land my first web developer job. Then the dot com bubble burst.

In 2006 I learned ActionScript which lead me on a very profitable journey to making beautiful interactive ads for AAA Movie titles and later video games. Then Steve Job declared death to Flash with his iPad.

In 2014 I learned AngularJS, MEAN stack, React and MERN stack and road another 10 years of OpenSource development.

In each of these transition the way it seemed things would always be, died. The birth of new technology is approachable by the masses at first. But if you watch closely a new complex set of desirable skills begins to emerge. This is that time with Agentic Software Development. 

At first it seems that all the common things we build can now be built in record time! No more 2 week sprints to get those few tasks completed. Short sight individuals will say we don’t need as many engineers or engineers at all. But a visionary says… Now that the basic stuff is out of the way, what can we build that’s never been built before? 

A new altitude had been unlocked ladies and gentlemen, where there was once financial barriers. Where companies couldn’t afford to build because the cost was greater than the return or where the risk was too costly is no more. Often dreams are never built because the cost or risk is too great. But now that POC can be built in a day and that 1 year MVP can be built in 2 weeks. I have personally experienced this. 

Let’s rise up! As if we’ve been given jet packs! We’re in a new layer! Grab a jet pack and build what was once impossible. The sprint load will return when companies allow their imagination to exceed 10x what they formerly thought was possible inside the quarterly roadmap.

BREAKING: Apple just proved AI "reasoning" models like Meta’s Llama, DeepSeek AI and OpenAI ‘s o3-mini don't actually reason at all.

They just memorize patterns really well.

(hint: we're not as close to AGI as the hype suggests)

——

Instead of using the same old math tests that AI companies love to brag about, Apple created fresh puzzle games.

They tested Claude Thinking, DeepSeek-R1, and o3-mini on problems these models had never seen before.

The result ↓

——

All "reasoning" models hit a complexity wall where they completely collapse to 0% accuracy.

No matter how much computing power you give them, they can't solve harder problems.

——

As problems got harder, these "thinking" models actually started thinking less.

They used fewer tokens and gave up faster, despite having unlimited budget.


——

Apple researchers even tried giving the models the exact solution algorithm.

Like handing someone step-by-step instructions to bake a cake.

The models still failed at the same complexity points.

They can't even follow directions consistently.

——

The research revealed three regimes:

• Low complexity: Regular models actually win
• Medium complexity: "Thinking" models show some advantage
• High complexity: Everything breaks down completely

Most problems fall into that third category.

——

Apple discovered that these models are not reasoning at all, but instead doing sophisticated pattern matching that works great until patterns become too complex.

Then they fall apart like a house of cards.

——

If these models were truly "reasoning," they should get better with more compute and clearer instructions.

Instead, they hit hard walls and start giving up.

Is that intelligence or memorization hitting its limits?

——

This research suggests we're not as close to AGI as the hype suggests.

Current "reasoning" breakthroughs may be hitting fundamental walls that can't be solved by just adding more data or compute.

——

Models could handle 100+ moves in Tower of Hanoi puzzles but failed after just 4 moves in River Crossing puzzles.

This suggests they memorized Tower of Hanoi solutions during training but can't actually reason.

——

While AI companies celebrate their models "thinking," Apple basically said "Everyone's celebrating fake reasoning."

The industry is chasing metrics that don't measure actual intelligence.

——

Apple's researchers used controllable puzzle environments specifically because:

• They avoid data contamination
• They require pure logical reasoning
• They can scale complexity precisely
• They reveal where models actually break

Smart experimental design if you ask me.
