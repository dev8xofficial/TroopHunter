Brazil denied me entry on arrival, forced me to cosplay Tom Hanks for 40 hours, and told me I had to go back to Portugal. Here's how I won 1st place and $2500 at a hackathon in Argentina anyways.

For ~1 week in November, there are events every day in Buenos Aires for DevConnect https://devconnect.org capped by an ETHGlobal hackathon with $500k in total prizes. I found that there was a second, much smaller, hackathon happening after Websummit and before ETHGlobal. So por que no los dos?

The 1st hackathon had around $20k in prizes and 5 tracks to choose from: https://lnkd.in/dnHkBTsQ

Websummit ended on Friday, but to make it to the hackathon on time, I left early - Thursday night from Lisbon.

We landed Thursday night in Brazil... but due to visa issues, I was denied entry in Fortaleza and told I will be put on the first flight back to Lisbon... The problem is that it would not be until Friday night. The federales argument was the slippery slope: "if we let him in, then he will tell others and soon everybody will come to Brazil without a visa!" Well, it is what it is.

Friday night, LATAM airlines gets me a ticket via TAP Portugal. After checking in, the TAP Portugal staff found me and my guard (I was under 24/7 guard...) eating pizza and literally took the ticket back. Basically, I became a radioactive passenger who no airlines wanted responsibility for, and the next LATAM Airlines flight might not be until Monday.

Saturday morning, the hackathon has already started but I'm told I will be sent to Lisbon via Paris, on Air France.. on Sunday night. I've already spent 2 nights on the airport terminal floor, being bitten by god knows what (mosquitos and bed bugs that I could never see - but the inflamed bites I find every morning when I wake up are evidence that it was not my imagination)

1 hour later after being given this news, the LATAM air official sits down and says "Do you still want to go to Argentina?" I can only assume that we crossed an invisible threshold where it became cheaper for them to find a way to my final destination rather than to detain me until Monday.

They put me on the next flight to Santiago, Chile. I found that the wifi on South American flights is shockingly good, with a stable fast connection even above the Andes mountains. Nevertheless, I still could only work on the hackathon from planes and airports. I would be spending Saturday night in the Chilean airport and not arrive in Argentina until a couple hours before the submission deadline.

With many disadvantages (less time, access, and prior knowledge) I knew that I would need to come up with a strategy to turn my competitors' strengths into their weakness.

I hit Linkedin's post length limit now, but will continue the saga if there is enough interest :) Like, comment and reshare if you want to see exactly how it ends.

Content, marketing, and editorial folks create the content that winds up on the web, and this product is for them as much as anyone.
