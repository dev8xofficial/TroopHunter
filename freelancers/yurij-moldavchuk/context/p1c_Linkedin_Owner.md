Boosting Development Efficiency with Upgraded Code Conventions

So what is the importance of rules and conventions in the way we write code? The immense. They serve as the guiding principles that shape our codebase and contribute to its long-term maintainability and scalability.

Undoubtedly, books like "Code Complete" by McConnell and "Clean Code" by Martin offer valuable insights and established conventions that are essential for projects likely to be passed on to other teams. However, when our team handles projects from start to finish and beyond, it becomes crucial to tailor our rules and conventions to our specific needs and preferences. This allows us to optimize our workflow and leverage our collective expertise to the fullest extent.

Two fundamental aspects must be kept in mind while implementing these upgraded covenants: enforcing them for everyone and ensuring they do not contradict one another.

But nobody likes rules! How to force developers to follow them? In general, this will have to be strictly monitored for about a month, until everyone gets used to it. But AIs such as GPT or Copilot have proven to be invaluable allies in this regard. After just a week of utilizing our upgraded rules, the AI itself actively reminds our developers of them, promoting adherence and streamlining the development process. This has resulted in a 30% increase in the team's productivity and efficiency.

Our custom code conventions not only provide a solid foundation for collaboration and knowledge sharing within our team but also foster a sense of ownership and empowerment among our developers. By creating an environment where everyone's ideas and expertise are valued, we have unlocked a new level of innovation and productivity.

In conclusion, while established books like "Code Complete" and "Clean Code" are highly valuable resources, they should serve as a starting point rather than a rigid set of rules for projects handled from start to finish. By developing our own upgraded code conventions, backed by AI-driven enforcement, we have witnessed significant improvements in development speed and overall quality.

React Native: Our Choice and Its Advantages

React Native offers cross-platform functionality, allowing code to run natively on both Android and iOS devices. With React Native, a significant percentage of code (90–100%) can be shared across platforms.

Using a unified design language system has allowed consistent component names, screen designs, and other elements across multiple platforms including Android, iOS, React Native, and web versions. This consistency enables writing cross-platform features easily.

The React framework is the most-loved web framework for many reasons: its simplicity, power, and scalability to large codebases. Components, simplified lifecycles, and declarative nature are among its most notable benefits.

Contrary to popular belief, moving business logic and layout off the main thread can improve render performance on mobile devices. In cases where performance issues occur, they are usually due to excessive rendering, which can be addressed through the effective use of shouldComponentUpdate, removeClippedSubviews, and better use of Redux.

The React Native Animated library allows for jank-free animations, including scrolling parallax and interaction-driven animations.

React Native leverages the vast array of JavaScript projects including Redux, Reselect, Jest, and others. Developers can take advantage of these open-source projects to create more robust applications.

The hype cycle model and where React sits within it.

React's journey:
- Innovation Trigger (2013): React launched, gained popularity for its unique features.
- Peak of Inflated Expectations (2014–2015): Wide discussion, excessive attention, illusions. Many poorly designed apps created.
- Disappointment Chasm (2016–2018): Limitations became apparent. Criticism mounted — not reactive enough, inconvenient for team development, forced rewrites.
- Slope of Enlightenment: Community understood benefits and drawbacks. The v16 release was the most significant turning point.
- Plateau of Productivity: React has proven viability and economic benefits. It is the de facto standard for building user interfaces, with a vast contributor community.

It happens that you take on a large client, and your company's profits depend on 50%+ of the work with this one client. If you're in this situation, change something immediately.

Yes, the temptation is great — everything is going well, the client is already in good relations with you, it's like a family. But not for business.

The client is just an ordinary person and may stop funding, or some family problem or natural disaster may happen. You may find yourself drawing from your reserves or dipping into your own pocket to cover employee salaries.

My advice if you haven't already found yourself in this situation:
- Don't put all your eggs in one basket — look for more clients so your income doesn't depend on one project.
- Invest in marketing and sales departments.
- Expand your staff by hiring people hourly to take on as many projects as possible without sticking to just one.
